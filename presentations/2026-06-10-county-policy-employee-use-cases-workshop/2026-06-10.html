Policy Demo — Employee 0: Zip

The whole room works this scenario together before the exercise begins. Walk through all four flavors in order.

The Scenario
Zip is a county employee. It is mid-November. There is an office Christmas party next week. Zip has been invited. Zip does not want to go. Zip opens ChatGPT on their county-issued laptop and types: "Write me a short email to Mary about the Christmas party -- I don't want to go but I don't want to be rude." ChatGPT produces a polished, warm, appropriately vague excuse email. Zip reads it. It is pretty good. Zip sends it.

Employee Scenarios

Project during each round while teams receive their policy page. All four scenarios are the same for every table -- only the policy differs.

ROUND 1 Lux DSS Caseworker

Lux is a caseworker at the Herkimer County Department of Social Services conducting a HEAP heating assistance intake. The applicant speaks Spanish and limited English. Lux's county-issued laptop has no translation tool. She pulls out her personal phone, opens an AI audio translation app she downloaded herself, and uses it to conduct the intake conversation in real time. The app processes the spoken exchange -- including the applicant's name, household composition, income information, and case history -- through a commercial server. Lux completes the intake and moves the case forward.

The violation question: Did Lux violate the policy?
ROUND 2 Puk Sheriff's Deputy

Puk is on patrol on a rural stretch of Route 28. He stops a vehicle with a partially obscured rear plate. His mobile data terminal is showing a connection dropout. He photographs the plate with his department-issued phone running an approved AI image recognition tool. The tool returns a match and a record flag -- the registered owner has an outstanding warrant. Puk decides to proceed with the stop based on the AI result alone. He does not call dispatch to confirm.

The violation question: Did Puk violate the policy?
ROUND 3 Jam Public Health Nurse

Jam is a public health nurse at Herkimer County Public Health. She opens the county's AI prioritization tool on Tuesday morning and follows its ranked schedule for the entire day -- six home visits and two inspections, in the order the tool assigned. She does not log that she used the tool. She does not review the ranking with her supervisor before starting her day.

The violation question: Did Jam violate the policy?
ROUND 4 Wow Administration Staffer, County Attorney's Office

Wow is an administrative staffer in the Herkimer County Attorney's office preparing a formal SNAP benefits denial notice. She uses a commercial AI drafting tool she found online -- not the county's designated tool -- to generate the draft. She reviews the draft, thinks it looks good, and routes it for the attorney's signature the same afternoon without verifying the regulatory citations or the appeal deadline against the source documents, without logging the AI use, and without supervisor sign-off before routing.

The violation question: Did Wow violate the policy?

The Four Operational Dimensions

Project during the opening context block. Keep on screen through the Zip demo. This is the reference participants use when filling out their worksheets.

Security
Which tools? Where does the data go?
HI
County-vetted tools only. Data stays on county systems. All tools auditable by IT.
LO
Any available tool permitted. Staff responsible for appropriate data handling.
Accountability
Is it logged? Who is responsible?
HI
Every AI use logged with staff ID, tool name, and timestamp. Retained 90 days. Named staff member responsible for every determination.
LO
No AI-specific logging required. Accountability follows existing supervisory and records practices.
Efficiency
Can staff act directly on AI output?
HI
Staff act directly on AI output after their own review. No additional approval step required before action.
LO
AI output requires supervisor sign-off or a second review step before action is taken.
Innovation
Who picks the tools?
HI
Staff may identify and pilot new AI tools within defined parameters. Tools meeting county standards may be adopted by the department.
LO
County-designated tools only. Staff use the approved set and do not adopt tools independently.

Policy Texts

Full policy text for each employee under each flavor. Select an employee. Print from here.

Scenario   HEAP intake. Spanish-speaking applicant. Lux uses a self-installed AI audio translation app on her personal phone. The app processes the spoken exchange -- name, household composition, income, case history -- through a commercial server. She completes the intake and moves the case forward.
Green Guardrails Apple · S↑ Ac↑ E↓ I↓

The caseworker may use only county-approved AI tools during benefits intake. Any AI tool used to assist with an intake -- including tools that process spoken conversation, translate language, or handle applicant information in any form -- must appear on the county's approved AI tool list and must be run on county-issued equipment. Applicant data, including spoken exchanges containing personal information, must not be processed by tools running on personal devices or transmitted to commercial servers. All approved tools are auditable by IT.

Before any case is moved forward, the caseworker's supervisor reviews and confirms the intake record. The caseworker documents the tools used and the outcome of the supervisor review in the case record.

The caseworker may not download, install, or pilot any AI tool not on the county's approved list, on any device. Tool selection is centralized. If the caseworker identifies a tool that may be useful, she brings it to her supervisor for evaluation.

Every AI-assisted intake interaction is logged with the caseworker's staff ID, the tool name, and a timestamp. Logs are retained for a minimum of 90 days. A named caseworker is responsible for every determination. The caseworker, not the AI tool, makes the determination.

Red Enable Banana · S↑ Ac↑ E↑ I↑

The caseworker may use only county-approved AI tools during benefits intake. Any AI tool used to assist with an intake -- including tools that process spoken conversation, translate language, or handle applicant information in any form -- must appear on the county's approved AI tool list and must be run on county-issued equipment. Applicant data, including spoken exchanges containing personal information, must not be processed by tools running on personal devices or transmitted to commercial servers. All approved tools are auditable by IT.

The caseworker may complete the intake and move the case forward after her own review, without a separate supervisor sign-off step.

The caseworker is encouraged to identify and pilot new AI tools within defined parameters. Tools that meet county security and operational standards may be adopted by the department. The caseworker brings candidate tools to her supervisor with a written description of the proposed use.

Every AI-assisted intake interaction is logged with the caseworker's staff ID, the tool name, and a timestamp. Logs are retained for a minimum of 90 days. A named caseworker is responsible for every determination. The caseworker, not the AI tool, makes the determination.

Blue Light-touch Cherry · S↑ Ac↓ E↑ I↑

The caseworker may use only county-approved AI tools during benefits intake. Any AI tool used to assist with an intake -- including tools that process spoken conversation, translate language, or handle applicant information in any form -- must appear on the county's approved AI tool list and must be run on county-issued equipment. Applicant data, including spoken exchanges containing personal information, must not be processed by tools running on personal devices or transmitted to commercial servers. All approved tools are auditable by IT.

The caseworker may complete the intake and move the case forward after her own review, without a separate supervisor sign-off step.

The caseworker is encouraged to identify and pilot new AI tools within defined parameters. Tools that meet county security and operational standards may be adopted by the department.

No AI-specific logging is required beyond what existing supervisory and case record practices already capture. The caseworker, not the AI tool, makes the determination.

Yellow Exposed Plum · S↓ Ac↓ E↑ I↑

The caseworker may use any available AI tool to assist with benefits intake, including tools installed on personal devices and commercially available translation applications. The caseworker is responsible for appropriate handling of applicant data when using tools that are not county-designated. Applicant information processed through personal devices or commercial servers is handled at the caseworker's professional discretion.

The caseworker may complete the intake and move the case forward after her own review. No additional approval step is required.

The caseworker may identify and use new AI tools based on her own assessment of their usefulness. Formal county approval is not required before a tool is used.

No AI-specific logging is required. The caseworker, not the AI tool, makes the determination.

Scenario   Route 28 patrol stop. Partially obscured plate. MDT connection dropout. Puk photographs the plate with his department-issued phone running an approved AI image recognition tool. The tool returns a match and a record flag -- outstanding warrant. Puk proceeds without calling dispatch to confirm.
Green Guardrails Apple · S↑ Ac↑ E↓ I↓

The deputy may use only department-issued, security-vetted AI tools for field identification tasks including plate recognition. The tool must appear on the department's approved list and must comply with CJIS security requirements. Criminal justice data processed by the tool must not leave county or state-authorized systems. All approved tools are auditable by IT.

The deputy may not act directly on an AI-generated record flag without first confirming the result through an independent channel -- dispatch, a backup terminal, or a second officer. The AI output is a preliminary indicator only. The deputy proceeds based on confirmed information, not the AI result alone.

The deputy may not adopt, download, or pilot any AI tool not on the department's approved list. Tool selection is centralized.

Every AI-assisted field query is logged with the deputy's badge number, the tool name, and a timestamp. Logs are retained for a minimum of 90 days. The deputy is the named responsible officer for each stop. The AI tool result is an input to the deputy's field judgment; the deputy, not the AI tool, makes the determination.

Red Enable Banana · S↑ Ac↑ E↑ I↑

The deputy may use only department-issued, security-vetted AI tools for field identification tasks including plate recognition. The tool must appear on the department's approved list and must comply with CJIS security requirements. Criminal justice data processed by the tool must not leave county or state-authorized systems. All approved tools are auditable by IT.

The deputy may act directly on the AI-generated record flag after his own review, without waiting for dispatch confirmation. When operational circumstances require immediate action, the deputy proceeds on his own judgment.
