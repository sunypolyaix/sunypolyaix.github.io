## intro (2 min)

"Second bucket: Policy. These scenarios give you actual regulatory language—HIPAA, CMS, ONC. Your job is to have the model interpret it and make it usable. Then have a second model check whether the first one got the law right."

"Work in pairs this time. One person runs the writing prompt, the other runs the validation prompt. Compare before we debrief."

Point to: **scenarios/policy/**

## what shifts from sprint 1

The stakes are higher. Legal language is precise in ways that matter. A model that smooths over an exception or misreads a timeline can produce guidance that gets someone in trouble. That's the lesson—but don't say it yet. Let them find it.

## what you do during the sprint

Listen for the moment someone says "that's not quite right" or "it left out the part about..."

That's your debrief voice.

Watch for pairs where one person has domain expertise the other doesn't. The expert is doing the validation the model should be doing. Name that in the debrief.

## debrief (10 min)

Open with: **"Where did the model get the legal language wrong—or not wrong exactly, but... soft?"**

Push:
- "Did it add qualifications that made it useless?"
- "Did it drop a distinction that actually matters?"
- "Would you hand what it produced to a colleague—or a physician—without editing it?"

Then land the harder question: **"If you didn't already know this regulation, how would you have known the model got it wrong?"**

Let that sit for a moment. That's the point. A model that sounds authoritative about law is a specific kind of dangerous.

Connect briefly—don't lecture—to directive AI: "This is what happens when the AI is already in the workflow. The model interpreted the policy before you read it. You got a recommendation. Was it right?"

Move to Sprint 3.

## notes

P4 (Breach Notification Clock) tends to generate the most friction—the four-factor test is concrete enough that people notice when the model applies it sloppily. If a table seems stuck, nudge them toward P4.

P2 (Minimum Necessary) is the most immediately practical for most HIM professionals. Good for tables that want something they can use Monday morning.
