# Round 2 — The Release of Information Policy

**The situation:** Your hospital's Release of Information (ROI) policy manual is
dense and dated. A new staff member asked a simple question — "Can I release
these records?" — and could not find the answer quickly. Your manager wants a
plain-language one-pager staff can actually use at the desk.

**Which model:** Use Claude or ChatGPT (free). Check the result with a second
model.

*The excerpt below is a realistic but fictional policy written for this
workshop. It is not your hospital's policy.*

---

## The document — paste this into your first model

> **Health Information Management Policy 7.4 — Release of Information**
> *(fictional, for training)*
>
> **Purpose.** To govern the disclosure of protected health information in
> response to requests from patients, providers, and third parties.
>
> **Authorization.** A valid written authorization is required before releasing
> records to a third party, except where disclosure is permitted or required by
> law. A valid authorization must identify the information to be disclosed, the
> recipient, the purpose, an expiration date or event, and the patient's
> signature and date.
>
> **Turnaround.** Routine requests are processed within 10 business days.
> Patient requests for their own records are fulfilled within 30 days, with one
> 30-day extension permitted if the patient is notified in writing.
>
> **Sensitive information.** Records involving behavioral health, substance use
> treatment, and HIV/AIDS status require additional, specific authorization and
> may not be released under a general authorization.
>
> **Fees.** Patients may be charged a reasonable, cost-based fee for copies.
> Fees may not be charged when records are sent to another provider for
> treatment.
>
> **Verification.** Staff must verify the identity of the requester and the
> validity of the authorization before any release.
