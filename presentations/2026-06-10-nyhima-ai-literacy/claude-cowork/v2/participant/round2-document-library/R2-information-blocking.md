# Round 2 — The Information Blocking Rule

**The situation:** Your compliance team wants staff to understand a key piece of
the information blocking rule. You need to turn the regulatory language into
something practical clinical staff can actually follow.

**Which model:** Use Claude or ChatGPT (free). Check the result with a second

---

## The document — paste this into your first model

> **45 CFR Part 171 — ONC Information Blocking Rule (excerpt)**
>
> "An actor engages in information blocking if the actor knows, or should know,
> that the practice is likely to interfere with, prevent, or materially
> discourage access, exchange, or use of electronic health information, unless
> the actor is a health care provider and the practice is required by law or
> covered by an exception."
>
> Exceptions (summary):
> - **Preventing Harm** — reasonable and necessary to prevent patient harm
> - **Privacy** — consistent with applicable privacy laws
> - **Security** — necessary to protect the security of health information
> - **Infeasibility** — fulfilling the request is not feasible
> - **Health IT Performance** — needed to maintain or improve the system
> - **Content and Manner** — responds in a different but permitted way
> - **Fees** — charges are reasonable and cost-based
> - **Licensing** — relates to licensing of interoperability elements
>
> Penalties: up to $1,000,000 per violation for health IT developers and
> information networks; separate disincentives for health care providers.
