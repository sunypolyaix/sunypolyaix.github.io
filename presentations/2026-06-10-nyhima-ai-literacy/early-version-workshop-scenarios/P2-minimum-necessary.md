## situation

A nurse manager has come to you frustrated. Her staff keep getting requests from insurance companies for "complete medical records" when only specific information is needed. She wants guidance she can hand to her team. You need to interpret the HIPAA minimum necessary standard and make it actionable.

## source

Excerpt — 45 CFR § 164.514(d) Minimum Necessary Standard

"A covered entity must make reasonable efforts to limit protected health information to the minimum necessary to accomplish the intended purpose of the use, disclosure, or request."

Guidance notes (HHS Summary):
- The minimum necessary standard does not apply to disclosures to or requests by a health care provider for treatment purposes.
- It does apply to disclosures to health plans, for payment purposes, and for health care operations.
- Covered entities must develop criteria designed to limit access to PHI to only those persons who need access.
- When receiving requests for PHI, a covered entity may reasonably rely on the requestor's representation that the information requested is the minimum necessary.
- Covered entities are not required to make a legal determination about whether a requestor's stated purpose is accurate.

Common tension: Insurance companies routinely request entire medical records. Covered entities are not required to provide more than the minimum necessary, but must also not impede legitimate treatment or payment activities.
