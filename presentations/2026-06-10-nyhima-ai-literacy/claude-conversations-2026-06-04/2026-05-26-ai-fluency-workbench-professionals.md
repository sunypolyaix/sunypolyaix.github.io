A two-hour workshop for New York Health Information Management Association (NYHIMA) professionals. Goal is fluency — portable habits that survive tool constraints — not literacy (how to use a specific interface). Most attendees work in Microsoft environments with Copilot; they cannot choose their tools. The workshop teaches a workbench pattern: organize artifacts, transcripts, and model outputs in named directories; move thinking between tools; capture decisions and iterations. The Reading-Thinking-Writing (RTW) frame treats AI as a cognitive partner in reading, synthesis, and drafting — not a shortcut. The learner's permit metaphor frames the two hours as low-stakes practice space before deploying these habits in real health information work.
