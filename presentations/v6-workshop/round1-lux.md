# Round 1 -- Lux, DSS Caseworker
## Team: Apple

**The scenario.** Lux is a caseworker at the Herkimer County Department of Social Services conducting a HEAP heating assistance intake. The applicant speaks Spanish and limited English. Lux's county-issued laptop has no translation tool. She pulls out her personal phone, opens an AI audio translation app she downloaded herself, and uses it to conduct the intake conversation in real time. The app processes the spoken exchange -- including the applicant's name, household composition, income information, and case history -- through a commercial server. Lux completes the intake and moves the case forward.

**The policy.**

The caseworker may use only county-approved AI tools during benefits intake. Any AI tool used to assist with an intake -- including tools that process spoken conversation, translate language, or handle applicant information in any form -- must appear on the county's approved AI tool list and must be run on county-issued equipment. Applicant data, including spoken exchanges containing personal information, must not be processed by tools running on personal devices or transmitted to commercial servers. All approved tools are auditable by IT.

Before any case is moved forward, the caseworker's supervisor reviews and confirms the intake record. The caseworker documents the tools used and the outcome of the supervisor review in the case record.

The caseworker may not download, install, or pilot any AI tool not on the county's approved list, on any device. Tool selection is centralized. If the caseworker identifies a tool that may be useful, she brings it to her supervisor for evaluation.

Every AI-assisted intake interaction is logged with the caseworker's staff ID, the tool name, and a timestamp. Logs are retained for a minimum of 90 days. A named caseworker is responsible for every determination. The caseworker, not the AI tool, makes the determination.
