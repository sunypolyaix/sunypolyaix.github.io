## 1.0 Introduction

### 1.1 Why Privacy Is Important

Many City services expose workforce members to private aspects of other people's lives during vulnerable times. Health records can include some of the most intimate details about a person's life, documenting physical and mental health, social behaviors, personal relationships, and financial status. When people use City services or receive City health insurance benefits, they trust the City to respect their privacy rights by maintaining the confidentiality and security of their personal information.

Reasons privacy, confidentiality, and security are important:

- Confidentiality is essential to build trusting relationships with individuals receiving care, often members of vulnerable communities who have experienced stigma and discrimination.
- Patients who believe that health services are confidential are more likely to seek care, particularly for reproductive health, mental health, or substance abuse matters.
- Giving an individual control over personal information encourages autonomy and active participation in managing one's own health care.
- Privacy violations and security breaches cause the individual to suffer financial harm, anxiety, and embarrassment, and in some cases may jeopardize immigration status, child custody, ability to obtain insurance coverage, employment eligibility, or access to benefits.

### 1.2 What Is HIPAA

Three interconnected concepts govern privacy and security of health records:

- **Privacy** refers to an individual's right to control personal matters and information. Privacy laws specify who can access personal information and under what conditions.
- **Confidentiality** focuses on preventing personal information exchanged in confidence from being divulged to third parties.
- **Security** involves procedural and technical measures that prevent unauthorized access to confidential information and protect the integrity and availability of electronically maintained records.

HIPAA (the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act of 1996, as amended by HITECH 2009) is a federal law establishing national standards for health information privacy and security. The HIPAA Rules include:

- **The Privacy Rule** — sets limits and conditions on uses and disclosures of an individual's personal health information without the individual's permission and gives individuals rights over their health information.
- **The Security Rule** — requires appropriate administrative, physical, and technical safeguards to ensure the confidentiality, integrity, and security of electronic protected health information.
- **The Breach Notification Rule** — requires HIPAA covered entities and their business associates to provide notification following a breach of unsecured protected health information.

When HIPAA and another law apply to the same records and their requirements conflict, the City must follow the law that is most protective of the individual.

### 1.3 Who Must Follow These Policies and Procedures

All workforce members within the City designated health care component are required to comply. This includes all workforce members of Covered Units, and any workforce members of Support Units who have access to protected health information.

### 1.4 Relationship to Other Policies

This Manual takes precedence over any departmental or unit-specific policies, procedures, or protocols that conflict with it. Covered Units are expected to develop and maintain the following unit-specific HIPAA protocols:

1. Designated Record Sets (Section 3.2)
2. Role-Based Access (Section 4.1)

4. Mandatory Reporting (Section 5.7)

6. Responding to Individual Requests for Correction — optional (Section 8.3)
7. Responding to Individual Requests for Restrictions or Confidential Communications — optional (Section 8.5)

## 2.0 Key Terms

### 2.1 Definitions

**Authorization** — a document signed and dated by the individual consenting to certain uses and disclosures of protected health information that contains all elements required by HIPAA (see Section 5.3).

**Breach** — the acquisition, access, use, or disclosure of protected health information in a manner that (1) is not permitted under the HIPAA Rules and (2) compromises the privacy or security of the PHI, as determined by a risk assessment conducted by the City HIPAA Privacy Officer.

**Business Associate** — any person or entity (outside of the City workforce) that:
- creates, receives, maintains, or transmits PHI on behalf of a Covered Unit to perform a function or activity regulated by HIPAA, including claims processing or administration, data analysis, processing or administration, utilization review, quality assurance, patient safety activities, billing, benefit management, practice management, or repricing;
- provides services to or for the Covered Unit involving access to PHI, including legal, actuarial, accounting, consulting, data aggregation, management, administrative, accreditation, or financial services; or
- is responsible for creating, receiving, maintaining, or transmitting a Covered Unit's PHI on behalf of a Support Unit or another Business Associate (i.e., a subcontractor).

**City PHI** — PHI that is transmitted or maintained by a Covered Unit, or by a Support Unit or business associate on behalf of a Covered Unit.

**Covered Entity** — an entity subject to HIPAA because it performs certain health care functions. The City is a covered entity for HIPAA compliance purposes. Because the City is a hybrid entity, only departments, divisions, units, and workforce members within the City's designated health care component are subject to HIPAA requirements.

**Covered Unit** — any City department, division, or unit that if standing alone, would be a covered entity. Also referred to as a "Health Care Component" in Executive Order No. 4-17: HIPAA Compliance Directive.

**Designated Health Care Component** — the portion of the City subject to HIPAA Rules, including all workforce members of Covered Units and any workforce members of Support Units who have access to City PHI.

**Designated Record Set** — a group of records maintained by or for a covered entity that may include patient medical and billing records; the enrollment, payment, claims, adjudication, and case or medical management record systems maintained by or for a health plan; or information used in whole or in part by a covered entity to make decisions about individuals' health care or health insurance benefits.

**Health Care Operations** — certain administrative, financial, legal, and quality improvement activities of a covered entity necessary to run its business, including:
- Conducting quality assessment and improvement activities, population-based activities, and case management;
- Reviewing competence or qualifications of health care professionals, evaluating performance, training, accreditation, certification, licensing, or credentialing;
- Underwriting and activities relating to the creation, renewal, or replacement of health insurance contracts;
- Conducting or arranging for medical review, legal, and auditing services, including fraud and abuse detection;
- Business planning and development;
- Business management and general administrative activities, including implementing and complying with the HIPAA Rules, customer service, resolution of internal grievances, creating de-identified health information, and fundraising.

**Health Care Provider** — a licensed provider of medical or health services (hospital, skilled nursing facility, home health agency, hospice, ambulance service, physician, nurse practitioner, physician assistant, nurse, physical therapist, psychologist, or clinical social worker), and any other person or organization who furnishes, bills, or is paid for health care in the normal course of business.

**Highly Confidential Information** — health information protected under HIPAA and more restrictive federal or state confidentiality laws, such as HIV-related information, substance abuse treatment records, mental health treatment records, and clinical laboratory results (see Section 3.3).

**HIPAA Documentation** — documentation related to HIPAA compliance and administration, including:
1. City HIPAA policies and procedures
2. Unit-specific and departmental HIPAA policies, procedures, or protocols
3. Any communication required by the HIPAA Rules
4. Any action, activity, or designation required to be documented by the HIPAA Rules
5. Incident documentation for any privacy and security incidents, and breach notification documentation
6. Business associate agreements with service providers and contractors

**HIPAA Rules** — the Privacy Rule, Security Rule, and Breach Notification Rule issued under HIPAA, set forth in 45 CFR Part 160 and Part 164.

**Incident** — any acquisition, access, use, or disclosure of PHI in a manner not permitted under the HIPAA Rules, other privacy laws, an applicable BAA or MOU, or City or unit-specific privacy or security policies. Encompasses situations that could potentially compromise the privacy or security of PHI, in addition to situations that are determined to actually compromise it.

**Individual** — the person who is the subject of protected health information.

**Marketing** — any communication about a product or service that encourages recipients to purchase or use the product or service, with certain exceptions (described at 45 CFR § 164.501).

**Payment** — the various activities of health care providers to obtain payment or be reimbursed for their services and of health plans to obtain premiums, to fulfill coverage responsibilities and provide benefits under the plan, and to obtain or provide reimbursement for the provision of health care. Examples include: determining eligibility or coverage under a plan and adjudicating claims; risk adjustments; reviewing health care services for medical necessity; billing and collection activities; and utilization review activities.

**Privacy Liaison** — the designated privacy official for a Support Unit.

**Privacy Officer** — the designated privacy official for a Covered Unit.

**Protected Health Information (PHI)** — information transmitted or maintained in any form or medium by a covered entity or a business associate on its behalf, that:
1. was created or received by a health care provider, health plan, employer, or health care clearinghouse;
2. relates to the physical or mental health or condition of an individual, the provision of health care to an individual, or the payment for the provision of health care to an individual; and
3. identifies the individual or might reasonably be used to identify the individual.

PHI excludes (1) education records covered by FERPA; (2) records described at 20 U.S.C. 1232g(a)(4)(B)(iv); (3) employment records held by a covered entity in its role as employer; and (4) information related to individuals deceased for more than 50 years.

**Support Units** — City departments, divisions, units, and workforce members that perform business, legal, financial, or administrative functions involving the use or disclosure of PHI on behalf of a Covered Unit.

**Treatment** — the provision, coordination, or management of health care and related services among health care providers or by a health care provider with a third party, consultation between health care providers regarding a patient, or the referral of a patient from one health care provider to another.

**Workforce Member** — an employee, volunteer, trainee, and other person whose conduct, in the performance of work for the City, is under the direct control of the City, whether or not that person is paid by the City.

## 3.0 Recognizing What Information Is Protected

### 3.1 The City's Hybrid Status

**Purpose**

HIPAA applies to "covered entities," which include health care providers that conduct certain types of transactions electronically, health plans, and health care clearinghouses. A single covered entity with a mix of HIPAA-covered and non-covered functions may designate itself a "hybrid entity," limiting compliance responsibilities to only the HIPAA-covered functions (the "health care component"). The hybrid entity must designate as part of its health care component:
- any department, division, or unit that if standing alone, would be a covered entity (a "Covered Unit"); and
- any unit and workforce member that performs business, legal, financial, or administrative services or functions involving the use or disclosure of PHI on behalf of a Covered Unit (a "Support Unit").

A hybrid entity is required to create adequate separation (firewalls) between the health care component and other components. PHI held by a Covered or Support Unit can only be disclosed to another part of the hybrid entity to the same extent HIPAA permits such a disclosure to a separate entity.

**Policy**

The City designated itself a hybrid entity in 2002, updated in Executive Order No. 4-17: HIPAA Compliance Directive.

- It does not disclose PHI to another department, division, or unit of the City, unless HIPAA would allow such disclosure to a legally separate entity.
- Each unit's electronic PHI is maintained separately and access is limited to that unit's workforce.
- Where possible, staff and office space are physically separated between covered and non-covered functions.
- If a person performs duties for both a Covered Unit and another City department, division, or unit, he or she cannot use or disclose PHI created or received in the course of working for the Covered Unit for other job duties.

Reference: 45 CFR § 164.105

### 3.2 Defining Protected Health Information and Designated Records Sets

**Purpose**

PHI includes information transmitted or maintained in any form or medium by a covered entity or a business associate on its behalf, that:
- Was created or received by or on behalf of a health care provider, health plan, employer, or health care clearinghouse;
- Relates to the physical or mental health or condition of an individual, the provision of health care to an individual, or the payment for the provision of health care to an individual; AND
- Identifies the individual or might reasonably be used to identify the individual.

PHI excludes (1) education records covered by FERPA; (2) records described at 20 U.S.C. 1232g(a)(4)(B)(iv); (3) employment records held by a covered entity in its role as employer; and (4) information related to individuals deceased for more than 50 years.

A patient's medical record and payment history are common examples of PHI, but PHI also includes demographic details (name, address, date of birth) and genetic characteristics (genetic testing, disorders, or predisposition to diseases). PHI even includes the fact that an individual has received services from a particular health care provider or is a member of a specific health plan.

**Policy**

City PHI includes any PHI transmitted or maintained by a Covered Unit, or by a Support Unit or business associate on behalf of a Covered Unit. Each Covered Unit must designate in writing its Designated Record Set(s):
- Health care providers (AHS, STDCP, EMS, PHL, and PNH) must include their medical and billing records and any other records used to make decisions about individuals.
- Health plans (DBHIDS and GHP) must include their enrollment, payment, claims adjudication, and case or medical management record systems, and any other records used to make decisions about individuals.

### 3.3 Highly Confidential Information

**Purpose**

Other federal and state laws contain additional protections for specific types of highly confidential health information, such as HIV-related information, substance abuse treatment records, mental health treatment records, and clinical laboratory results.

**Policy**

The City will follow HIPAA and all federal or state laws providing privacy protections for PHI and Highly Confidential Information. If state law is contrary to HIPAA, HIPAA will be followed except in cases where state law: provides greater privacy protections or privacy rights with respect to PHI; provides for the reporting of disease or injury, child abuse, birth, or death, or for public health surveillance; or requires certain health plan reporting.

As a general rule, Highly Confidential Information will not be disclosed without the individual's written consent specifically identifying the type of Highly Confidential Information to be disclosed. Exceptions include:
- Disclosures to other health care providers when knowledge of the Highly Confidential Information is necessary to provide emergency treatment to the patient.
- PHI required to be disclosed by a court order (see Section 5.8).

When disclosing HIV-Related Information, Substance Abuse Treatment Records, or Mental Health Treatment Records, the following notice must be provided to the recipient:

> "This information has been disclosed to you from records whose confidentiality is protected by Pennsylvania law (35 P.S. § 7607, 50 P.S. § 7111) and/or federal rules (42 CFR Part 2). These rules prohibit you from making any further disclosure of this information unless further disclosure is expressly permitted by the written consent of the person to whom it pertains or is otherwise authorized by the applicable law. A general authorization for the release of medical or other information is NOT sufficient for this purpose. The federal rules restrict any use of the information to criminally investigate or prosecute any alcohol or drug abuse patient."

### 3.4 De-Identified Data and Limited Data Sets

**Purpose**

Once PHI has been de-identified, it is no longer PHI, and the restrictions and requirements of federal and state privacy laws no longer apply. HIPAA authorizes two methods of de-identification: (a) use of statistical methods proven to render information not individually identifiable, and (b) deletion of 18 specified identifiers.

A limited data set may be used in certain situations when de-identified data is not sufficient for research, public health, or health care operations purposes, pursuant to a data use agreement. Since it is only partially de-identified, a limited data set is still considered PHI and remains subject to all HIPAA protections.

**Policy**

Unless an exception is granted by the CHPO, the only approved method for de-identification of City PHI is removal of all 18 identifiers listed in Table 3.4.

**Table 3.4: De-Identification Procedure**

**Step 1 — Remove these data elements:**
1. Names
2. All geographic subdivisions smaller than a State, including street address, city, county, precinct, zip code, and their equivalent geocodes (except the initial three digits of a zip code if the geographic unit formed by combining all zip codes with the same three initial digits contains more than 20,000 people). Currently, 036, 059, 063, 102, 203, 556, 592, 790, 821, 823, 830, 831, 878, 879, 884, 890, and 893 are all recorded as "000".
3. All elements of dates (except year) for dates directly related to an individual, including birth date, admission date, discharge date, date of death; and all ages over 89
4. Telephone numbers
5. Fax numbers
6. Electronic mail addresses
7. Social security numbers
8. Medical record numbers
9. Health plan beneficiary numbers
10. Account numbers
11. Certificate/license numbers
12. Vehicle identifiers and serial numbers, including license plate numbers
13. Device identifiers and serial numbers
14. Web Universal Resource Locators (URLs)
15. Internet Protocol (IP) address numbers
16. Biometric identifiers, including finger and voice prints
17. Full face photographic images and any comparable images
18. Any other unique identifying number, characteristic, or code

**Step 2 — Confirm that:** to your knowledge, the remaining information cannot be used alone or in combination with other information to identify an individual who is a subject of the information.

Reference: 45 CFR §§ 164.502(d), 164.514

## 4.0 Ground Rules for Working with PHI

### 4.1 The Minimum Necessary Standard

**Policy**

Workforce members may use, disclose, and request only the minimum amount of PHI necessary to accomplish a permitted purpose. Minimum necessary means that the smallest amount of PHI is available to the fewest people needed to get the job done.

The minimum necessary standard applies when: (a) using PHI internally; (b) disclosing PHI to anyone outside of the Covered or Support Unit; and (c) requesting PHI from any other Covered or Support Unit, or another covered entity.

Departmental management will authorize role-based access to PHI, limited to what is needed based on the scope and responsibilities of a workforce member's position.

**Table 4.1: Minimum Necessary Reference Guide**

| Recipient | Purpose | What to Disclose |
|---|---|---|
| Health care provider* | For treatment | May disclose whatever the health care provider requests |
| Individual (subject of the PHI)* | Any purpose | Must disclose what the Individual requests (with limited exceptions per Section 8.2) |
| Person named in an Authorization* | As stated in the Authorization | May disclose the PHI identified in the Authorization |
| Secretary of DHHS* | HIPAA compliance and enforcement | Must disclose the PHI requested by DHHS |
| Person specified by law or court order* | As required by law | May disclose whatever PHI is required for compliance |
| Public official (police, national security, public health authorities)** | Purposes permitted under 45 CFR § 164.512 | May disclose the PHI requested, if the official represents it is the minimum necessary for the stated purpose |
| Another HIPAA covered provider or health plan** | For payment or health care operations | May disclose the PHI requested |
| Researcher** | For approved research | May disclose the PHI indicated in the IRB approved Waiver of Authorization (see Section 5.9) |

*Exception to the minimum necessary standard.
**If reasonable under the circumstances, may rely on the request as representing the minimum necessary PHI.

Reference: 45 CFR § 164.502(b); § 164.514(d)

### 4.2 Verifying the Recipient

**Policy**

When making disclosures of PHI, workforce members must take reasonable steps to verify: (a) the identity of the person to whom the PHI is disclosed; and (b) that person's authority to receive the PHI.

**Procedures**

*Identity Check* — Acceptable methods include:
- In Person: valid driver's license, passport, employee or military ID, or other official photo identification. If the requestor is a public official or law enforcement, request an agency badge or other official credentials.
- Written Requests: match between signature on request and signature on file; provision of identifying information that matches individual's record; request on official letterhead or received from an official email address.
- Verbal Requests: confirmation of identifying data such as full name, date and place of birth, or other personal information unique to the individual.

*Authority Check* — Required documentation:
- Request by Parent or Guardian of a Minor Child: copy of a birth certificate, court order, guardianship documents, or other evidence of parental relationship.
- Request by Personal Representative: copy of a valid power of attorney, letters testamentary, appointment as executor, or other documents listed in Table 8.1.
- Request by Public Official: a written statement of the legal authority under which the information is requested.
- Request by Health Care Provider Treating the Individual: the requesting entity should provide the provider's name, facility name, location, and telephone number.
- Request by Other Third Parties: a copy of a valid Authorization naming that person.

Reference: 45 CFR § 164.514(h)

### 4.3 Tracking Disclosures

**Policy**

Individuals have the right to know who has received their PHI when they didn't authorize the disclosure or it was made for reasons other than treatment, payment, or health care operations. HIPAA requires covered entities to maintain records of all disclosures unless the disclosure fits within an exception.

The following categories of disclosures are EXCLUDED from the accounting:
- For purposes of treatment, payment, or health care operations
- Pursuant to the individual's Authorization
- To the individual or the individual's personal representative
- To persons involved in the individual's care or payment for care, including notification in disaster relief situations
- For national security or intelligence purposes
- To correctional institutions or law enforcement officials regarding inmates or individuals in lawful custody
- Incidental to another acceptable disclosure
- As part of a limited data set

**Procedures**

The tracking system must capture at least the following for each disclosure:
- Date of disclosure
- Recipient's name and, if known, recipient's address
- Description (what PHI was disclosed)
- Purpose (why PHI was disclosed)
- Name of individual(s) whose PHI was disclosed

Reference: 45 CFR § 164.528

## 5.0 Permitted Uses and Disclosures

### 5.1 Permitted Uses

**Purpose**

"Use" refers to internal activities that require sharing, application, review, or analysis of PHI within the entity that maintains the PHI. Due to the City's hybrid status, any activity that requires PHI to be shared from one Covered or Support Unit to another City unit is considered a disclosure rather than a use of PHI.

**Policy**

Covered Units may use PHI without the individual's written Authorization for:
- To provide treatment
- To pay or obtain payment for health care services
- For the Covered Unit's own health care operations
- As required by law
- For public health activities (if the Covered Unit is also a public health authority)
- For research, subject to the requirements of an approved Waiver of Authorization
- To create a limited data set or to de-identify data (see Section 3.4)

Special types of information:
- Genetic information may not be used for underwriting purposes.
- Psychotherapy notes may only be used for treatment purposes by the author of the notes, and in limited additional situations (consult the CHPO).

Reference: 45 CFR §§ 164.502, 164.508(a)(2)

### 5.2 Permitted Disclosures

**Policy**

A "disclosure" occurs when any PHI is accessed by or provided to a person or entity outside of the covered entity that created or is maintaining the PHI.

Permitted Disclosures:
- As authorized in writing by the Individual (see Section 5.3)
- When required by a court order (see Section 5.8)
- Without obtaining an Authorization or court order, PHI may also be disclosed in compliance with the requirements for each specific type of disclosure outlined in Sections 5.4-5.10

Mandatory Disclosures:
- To the individual who exercises his or her rights (see Chapter 8)
- To DHHS when required for HIPAA compliance purposes

Prohibited Disclosures:
- Genetic information for underwriting purposes
- Where the Covered or Support Unit provides PHI in exchange for money or other benefits qualifying as a "sale of PHI" under HIPAA
- Where the disclosure does not fit within a category of disclosures permitted by HIPAA
- Where other applicable laws or restrictions prohibit the disclosure

Reference: 45 CFR § 164.502

### 5.3 Consent/Authorization

**Policy**

City workforce members will obtain a valid Authorization from the individual or the individual's personal representative before disclosing PHI, unless this HIPAA Policy Manual specifically permits otherwise.

Individuals may refuse to authorize certain uses or disclosures of their PHI. Covered Units will not condition treatment, payment, enrollment, or benefits eligibility on an individual granting an Authorization, except:
- A health care provider may condition research-related treatment on provision of an Authorization for use or disclosure of PHI for such research.
- A health plan may condition enrollment on provision of an Authorization for PHI needed for eligibility or enrollment determinations or underwriting.
- A covered entity may require an Authorization before providing a service when the sole purpose of the service is to provide PHI to a third party.

An individual may revoke an Authorization at any time by written request.

**Table 5.3: Checklist of Required Elements in a HIPAA Authorization**

- What health information will be disclosed
- Who will disclose the information (must name the Covered or Support Unit releasing the record)
- Who will receive the information
- The purpose(s) for disclosing the information
- A statement informing the individual of: (1) his or her right to revoke the Authorization in writing, (2) how to revoke the Authorization, and (3) any exceptions to the right to revoke
- A statement that the covered entity cannot require the individual to sign the Authorization in order to receive treatment or payment or to enroll or be eligible for benefits
- A statement that the information may potentially be redisclosed by the recipient and no longer protected by the federal privacy regulations
- A statement that the Authorization will expire on a specific date, after a specific amount of time, or upon the occurrence of some event related to the patient
- The signature of the individual and the date (or personal representative, or two witnesses if physically unable to sign)
- Additional elements are required if Highly Confidential Information will be disclosed — consult Law

If the individual is a minor (under 18), a parent or legal guardian must sign the Authorization, except the following individuals who can authorize release of their own PHI:
- An emancipated minor (age 16 or older who has left the parental household, supports him/herself financially, and lives independently)
- A minor who has: married, been pregnant, or graduated from high school
- Any minor regarding treatment for pregnancy, sexually transmitted diseases, reproductive health/contraceptives, or substance abuse
- A minor age 14 or older regarding mental health treatment records

Reference: 45 CFR § 164.508; 42 CFR § 2.31(a)

### 5.4 Family and Others Involved in the Individual's Care

**Policy**

Covered Units may make the following disclosures of PHI (except for Highly Confidential Information) without obtaining the individual's written Authorization:
- To a family member, other relative, close personal friend, or any other person identified by the individual who is assisting the individual with health care or payment for care.
- To a family member, a personal representative, or another person responsible for the care of the individual, as needed to provide notification of the individual's location, general condition, or death.
- To a public or private entity authorized by law or charter to assist in disaster relief efforts, for notification purposes in emergency circumstances.
- To a family member or other persons who were involved in a deceased individual's care or payment for health care prior to the individual's death.

Workforce members will give the individual an opportunity to agree or object to disclosures made under this policy whenever possible.

Reference: 45 CFR § 164.510(b)

### 5.5 Treatment, Payment, and Health Care Operations

**Policy**

HIPAA permits covered entities to share PHI for certain treatment, payment, or health care operations activities without the individual's written Authorization.

Covered Units may share PHI without an Authorization for **treatment** activities:
- A Covered Unit may disclose PHI to any health care provider for that provider's treatment of the individual.
- Health care provider Covered Units (AHS, EMS, PNH, and STDCP) may disclose PHI to another agency or provider if needed for the Covered Unit's treatment of the individual.
- PHL is very limited by its licensing laws and can only disclose lab results to the physician who ordered the test and the individual who was tested.

Covered Units may share PHI without an Authorization for **payment** activities:
- A Covered Unit may disclose PHI to the extent needed for its own payment activities.
- A Covered Unit may disclose PHI to another covered entity or a health care provider for the payment activities of the receiving entity or provider.

Covered Units may share PHI without an Authorization for **health care operations** activities:
- A Covered Unit may disclose PHI for its own health care operations activities, to a Support Unit or business associate.
- A Covered Unit may disclose PHI to another Covered Unit or covered entity if each entity has or had a relationship with the individual, the PHI pertains to the relationship, and the disclosure is for a quality-related activity or for health care fraud and abuse detection.

Reference: 45 CFR § 164.506

### 5.6 Business Associates

**Policy**

A Covered Unit may disclose PHI to a business associate when authorized to do so by a fully executed written agreement between the parties that contains the mandatory BAA language approved by the Law Department.

A business associate is any person or entity (outside of the City workforce) that: creates, receives, maintains, or transmits PHI on behalf of a Covered Unit to perform a function or activity regulated by HIPAA; provides services to or for the Covered Unit involving access to PHI; or is responsible for creating, receiving, maintaining, or transmitting a Covered Unit's PHI on behalf of a Support Unit or another business associate.

**Procedures**

Before engaging a contractor to perform any services or functions involving access to City PHI, workforce members will work with their contract/conformance manager and the Law Department to determine whether the contractor is a business associate and, if so, ensure that an agreement containing appropriate BAA language is executed.

Business associate contracts must be maintained for a period of at least six years after the date the contract is no longer in effect.

The Privacy Officer/Liaison of each Covered or Support Unit is responsible for maintaining a list of all the unit's business associates, including: name, contact information, type of services or functions provided, effective date and term of underlying contract, date BAA was fully executed, and name of workforce member responsible for the relationship.

Reference: 45 CFR §160.103, 164.308(b), 164.314, 164.502(e), 164.504(e)

### 5.7 Public Health and Safety Reporting

**Policy**

A Covered or Support Unit may authorize its workforce members to disclose PHI without the individual's Authorization for the following **public health activities**:
- Reporting to a public health authority authorized by law to collect or receive such information for the purpose of preventing or controlling disease, injury, or disability.
- Reporting to someone under the jurisdiction of the FDA regarding the quality, safety, or effectiveness of regulated products and activities.
- Notifying individuals that they may have been exposed to a communicable disease.
- Providing proof of immunization to a school.
- Certain occupational health reporting to an employer.

A Covered or Support Unit may authorize its workforce members to disclose PHI to a public health or other government authority authorized by law to receive **reports of abuse, neglect, or domestic violence**:
- Workforce members who are mandated reporters under Pennsylvania's CPSL or OAPSA may disclose PHI to the extent required to meet their legal obligations.
- Beyond what is required by law, workforce members may disclose PHI to report abuse, neglect, or domestic violence if: (1) the individual agrees; (2) a health care professional believes the disclosure is necessary to prevent additional harm; or (3) in an emergency situation when the individual is unable to agree.

When reporting suspected abuse, neglect, or domestic violence (other than child abuse), the individual whose PHI was disclosed must be notified, except: when the personal representative is responsible for the abuse, or when notification would place the individual at risk of serious harm.

Reference: 45 CFR § 164.512 (a), (b), (c), (f)(1)(i), and (g); 23 Pa.C.S. § 6311, 35 P.S. § 10225.101 et seq.

### 5.8 Court Orders, Subpoenas, and Warrants

**Policy**

Requests for PHI pursuant to legal process are grouped into three categories:

**Category A — Court orders signed by a judge, magistrate, or administrative tribunal** (includes court-ordered warrants, subpoenas or summons signed by a judge, and grand jury subpoenas): The Covered or Support Unit may disclose only the PHI that is expressly ordered to be disclosed.

**Category B — Subpoenas or other discovery requests issued in the course of a judicial or administrative proceeding** (signed by a person other than a judge): PHI will not be disclosed unless the Covered or Support Unit has satisfied ONE of the following:
- Contacted the individual whose PHI is being requested in writing and explained the subpoena
- Obtained satisfactory written assurances from the entity issuing the subpoena
- Obtained a valid HIPAA Authorization from the individual

**Category C — Law enforcement demands** (administrative subpoenas, summons, investigative demands): The Covered or Support Unit may comply with the request if the issuing entity confirms that: (a) the information sought is relevant and material to a legitimate law enforcement inquiry; (b) the request is specific and limited to the extent reasonably necessary; and (c) de-identified information could not reasonably be used.

Before a disclosure of PHI is made under this Section, the following approvals are required:
- Disclosures from Covered Units will be approved by the unit's Privacy Officer or the CHPO.
- Disclosures from Support Units will be approved by the CHPO or the Privacy Officer of the Covered Unit whose PHI was requested.
- Any disclosure of Highly Confidential Information will be approved by the CHPO or the Law Department's Health and Adult Services Unit.

Per City policy, any person attempting to serve a Covered or Support Unit with a court order, warrant, subpoena, or summons will be directed to the Law Department, 1515 Arch Street, 14th Floor.

No original records may be released to any person or entity without receiving written approval from the Law Department.

Reference: 45 CFR § 164.512 (e) and (f)(1)(ii)

### 5.9 Research

**Policy**

City PHI may be used and disclosed for research purposes in several ways:
- As permitted in a HIPAA-compliant Authorization (see Section 5.3)
- In the form of a Limited Data Set under a data use agreement (see Section 3.2)
- As permitted in a modified Authorization approved by the IRB (an "Alteration")
- Under a Waiver of Authorization approved by the IRB (a "Waiver")
- For research solely involving the PHI of deceased individuals
- For certain activities preparatory to research

Clinical research involving City PHI requires the written approval of the head of any participating Covered Unit.

The Philadelphia Department of Public Health IRB is authorized to approve Alterations or Waivers involving the use or disclosure of City PHI for research.

A valid Alteration or Waiver must include: the identity of the approving IRB; the date on which the waiver or alteration was approved; a statement that the IRB has determined that all the specified criteria were met; a brief description of the PHI for which use or access has been determined necessary; a statement that the waiver or alteration was reviewed and approved under normal or expedited review procedures; and the required signature of the IRB chair or the chair's designee.

**Procedures**

The use or disclosure of PHI for the research project may not begin until written approval is provided by the IRB. Disclosures for research must be tracked as required by Section 4.3. Documentation related to Waivers and Alterations will be retained by the IRB for at least six years after the date the study was completed.

Reference: 45 CFR § 164.512(i), 45 CFR §§ 46.116 and 46.117

### 5.10 Other Reasons for Disclosing PHI

**Policy**

Covered Units may disclose PHI without the individual's Authorization, after appropriate review and approval by the unit's Privacy Officer, for the following activities and purposes:

- **To a Health Oversight Agency for Health Oversight Activities** — activities necessary for appropriate oversight of the health care system, government benefit programs, or entities subject to regulatory programs.
- **To a Law Enforcement Official for Law Enforcement Purposes** — under specified conditions, including when the subject is a victim of a crime who agrees to the disclosure, for identifying or locating suspects or missing persons, and in emergency situations.
- **For Cadaveric Organ, Eye or Tissue Donation Purposes** — to organ procurement organizations or other entities engaged in the procurement, banking, or transplantation of organs.
- **To Avert a Serious Threat to Health or Safety** — upon a good faith belief that the use or disclosure is necessary to prevent or lessen a serious and imminent threat.
- **For Specialized Government Functions** — including disclosures to correctional institutions and authorized federal officials for the conduct of national security activities.
- **For Workers' Compensation Programs** — to the extent necessary to comply with applicable laws.

Any use or disclosure of PHI for a marketing or fundraising purpose requires the individual's Authorization, unless the CHPO reviews and approves an exception.

Reference: 45 CFR §§ 164.502(j)(2), 164.508(a)(3), 164.512(d), (f), (h), (j), (k), & (l), 164.514(f)

## 6.0 Safeguarding Protected Health Information

**Policy**

All workforce members will follow these procedures in handling PHI to prevent unauthorized uses and disclosures, to limit incidental uses and disclosures, and to meet the Minimum Necessary Standard. An incidental use or disclosure is a secondary use or disclosure that cannot reasonably be prevented, is limited in nature, and occurs as a by-product of an otherwise permitted use or disclosure.

**Procedures**

**Safeguards for the Physical Environment**

- Bulletin boards in areas visible to individuals or visitors may not contain any PHI unless all individuals whose PHI appears have authorized the display.
- Computer screens must be positioned so that only authorized users can read the display. Displays will be configured to go blank when left unattended for more than a brief period. Computer screens left unattended for longer periods will log off the user.
- Conversations concerning PHI must be conducted in a way that reduces the likelihood of being overheard.
- Desks, countertops, printers, and fax machines — measures will be taken to prevent individuals and visitors from seeing information inappropriately.
- Sign-in sheets may be used only when necessary for patient care and efficient operations. Sign-in sheets must not include any information other than the patients' names.

**Safeguards for Transmitting PHI by Telephone, Fax, and Mail**

- All faxes containing PHI must be accompanied by a cover sheet with a confidentiality notice.
- Fax numbers should be double checked before and after sending.
- When sending PHI in the mail, the address must be double checked and the envelope must be securely sealed.
- Disclosures of PHI via telephone are only permitted in limited circumstances.
- Telephone messages may be left on answering machines unless the individual has requested alternative means of communication. Messages should not contain information that links a patient's name to a particular medical condition, provides results or any other clinical information, or references any Highly Confidential Information.

**Safeguards for Record Maintenance and Disposal**

- Areas where clinical records and other documents containing PHI are stored must be secure.
- All documents and materials containing PHI should be shredded when no longer needed.
- Before disposing of equipment capable of storing PHI, security procedures must be followed to ensure the PHI has been completely destroyed.

**Safeguards for Computers and Mobile Devices**

- Workforce members are prohibited from sharing passwords or any other log-in credentials.
- Workforce members may not use personal email accounts to receive, maintain, or transmit PHI.
- Workforce members may not copy or download PHI to a non-City storage location such as a hard drive, CD, DVD, flash drive, laptop, phone, or other storage device.
- Any laptop or mobile device used to create, access, or store PHI must be encrypted.

Reference: 45 CFR § 164.530(c)

## 7.0 Reporting Incidents

**Policy**

All workforce members are required to promptly report any suspected privacy or security incident. An "incident" is any acquisition, access, use, or disclosure of PHI in a manner not permitted by: the HIPAA Rules or other privacy laws; an applicable business associate agreement or MOU with a Support Unit; City privacy policies and procedures; or unit-specific privacy policies, procedures, or protocols.

The CHPO will determine whether an incident is a HIPAA "Breach": an acquisition, access, use, or disclosure of PHI in a manner not permitted by HIPAA, that compromises the privacy or security of the PHI, as determined by a risk assessment conducted by the CHPO.

**Procedures**

1. **Mandatory Duty to Report** — Any workforce member who suspects or becomes aware of an incident must immediately (no later than the next business day) notify his/her supervisor or unit manager. A completed Incident Report Form must be provided to the unit's Privacy Officer/Liaison no later than two business days after notifying the supervisor.

2. **Investigation** — The Privacy Officer/Liaison will lead an investigation, gathering evidence and performing interviews to determine the facts (what, where, when, who, why, how) and identify actions needed to mitigate any harmful effects.

3. **Risk Assessment** — No later than five business days after becoming aware of an incident, the Privacy Officer/Liaison will provide a completed Incident Report to the CHPO. The CHPO will conduct a risk assessment analyzing: the nature and extent of the PHI involved; the unauthorized person who used the PHI; whether the PHI was actually acquired or viewed; and the extent to which the risk has been mitigated.

4. **Mitigation and Corrective Action** — If the incident is a Breach, the CHPO will notify the Breach Response Team, which will determine appropriate mitigation steps and identify root causes.

5. **Breach Notification** — Notifications to affected individuals and the media (when required) will include: a brief description of what happened; the date of the breach and the date it was discovered; the types of PHI involved; steps the individual should take to protect themselves; a brief description of what the City is doing to investigate; and a toll-free number, email address, or postal address for follow-up questions.

**Table 7.0: Breach Notification**

| Breach Involving | Notification to the Individual | Notification to the Secretary of DHHS | Notification to the Media |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fewer than 500 people | Required without unreasonable delay, no later than 60 days after discovery | Required electronically, no later than 60 days after the close of the previous calendar year | Not required |
| 500 or more people | Same as above | Required electronically as soon as possible, no later than 60 days after the breach was discovered | Must be provided in major print or broadcast media where the affected individual(s) likely reside, no later than 60 days after discovery |

Reference: 45 CFR § 164.402, 164.404, 164.406, 164.408

## 8.0 Honoring an Individual's Rights

HIPAA gives individuals rights to control their own health information, including the right to get a copy of the information, make sure it is correct, and know who has seen it. These rights can also be exercised by a personal representative authorized to make decisions on behalf of the individual.

### 8.1 Right to Designate a Personal Representative

**Policy**

A personal representative will be given the same authority and rights as the individual to whom the PHI pertains, to the extent the personal representative is legally authorized to make health care decisions on behalf of the individual, or to act on behalf of a deceased individual or the deceased individual's estate.

Exceptions — a person will NOT be treated as a personal representative when:
- It is reasonably believed that an individual has been or may be subjected to domestic violence, abuse, or neglect by the personal representative.
- It is reasonably believed that treating such person as the personal representative could endanger the individual.
- A licensed health care professional determines, in the exercise of professional judgment, that it is not in the best interest of the individual to treat the person as the personal representative.

Special considerations for minors:
- An emancipated minor will be given the same authority and rights as an adult for HIPAA purposes.
- A parent, guardian, or other person acting in loco parentis is usually the personal representative of an unemancipated minor.
- An unemancipated minor will be given the same authority and rights as an adult in the following situations: when state or other law allows a minor to consent to a particular health care service; when someone other than the parent is authorized by law to consent; or when a parent agrees to a confidential relationship between the minor and a health care provider.

**Table 8.1: Documentation Requirements for a Personal Representative**

| If the Individual Is | The Personal Representative Is | Required Documentation |
|---|---|---|
| An Adult or Emancipated Minor | A person with legal authority to make health care decisions | Health care power of attorney; Court appointed legal guardianship papers; General or durable power of attorney that includes health care decisions |
| An Unemancipated Minor | A parent, guardian, or other person acting in loco parentis | Adult identified as parent sharing same address; documentation of guardianship; child listed as dependent on adult's health insurance |
| Deceased | A person with legal authority to act on behalf of the decedent or the estate | Executor: letters testamentary, court order, or will; Next of kin: proof of relationship (consult Law Department) |

Reference: 45 CFR § 164.502(g)

### 8.2 Right to Access, Inspect, and Obtain a Copy

**Policy**

Covered Units will provide individuals with access to their PHI upon request. Certain types of PHI will not be provided, including: PHI not part of the Covered Unit's designated record set; psychotherapy notes; or information compiled in anticipation of civil, criminal, or administrative action.

Covered Units may deny a request for access on unreviewable grounds (including when the PHI is not subject to the right to access, when involving an inmate under direction of a correctional institution, or during an active research clinical trial) or reviewable grounds (when a licensed health care professional determines access is reasonably likely to endanger the life or physical safety of the individual or another person).

When a request is denied, the individual will be sent a denial notice including: the basis for the denial; an explanation of the individual's rights to request a review; and a description of how the individual may make a complaint.

Covered Units will comply with the following timelines:
- Within 30 days of the date of the request, the Covered Unit must provide access, request an extension, or issue a notice of denial for information maintained onsite.
- One extension of up to 30 days is permitted for information maintained offsite.

The Covered Unit will not charge more than a flat copying fee of $6.50 per request, unless an alternate fee schedule has been approved by the CHPO.

Reference: 45 CFR § 164.524

### 8.3 Right to Correct Errors

**Policy**

Any Covered or Support Unit that maintains a designated record set will allow individuals to request in writing that a correction be made to their records. Requests for correction will be accepted and the appropriate amendment made, except when the information is: accurate and complete; was not created by the unit to whom the request was made; is not part of the designated record set; or is not subject to the individual's right to access.

Requests will be processed and action will be taken within 60 days of receiving the request. If needed, one 30-day extension is permitted.

No original record will be deleted or altered. A correction will be made by identifying the information that is erroneous or incomplete, supplying the corrected information in a dated amendment, and providing a reference or link to the location of the amendment.

Reference: 45 CFR § 164.526

### 8.4 Right to Know How Health Information Is Used or Disclosed

**Policy**

Each Covered Unit will maintain a Notice of Privacy Practices (NPP) that has been approved by the CHPO and describes: how the Covered Unit may use and disclose PHI; the individual's rights with respect to their PHI; the Covered Unit's legal duties with respect to PHI; and who individuals can contact for further information about the Covered Unit's privacy policies.

Health plans (DBHIDS and GHP) will: provide a copy of the NPP to new enrollees at the time of enrollment; provide a revised NPP to all individuals covered by the plan within 60 days of any material revision; and notify all individuals covered by the plan that they can obtain a copy at least once every three years.

Direct health care providers (AHS, EMS, STDCP, and PNH) will: provide a copy of the NPP to the individual no later than the first date of service; in an emergency, provide the NPP as soon as reasonably practicable after the emergency has ended.

Covered Units will provide an individual, upon request, with an accounting of all disclosures required by HIPAA to be included in such an accounting.

Reference: 45 CFR §§ 164.520, 164.528

### 8.5 Right to Limit Certain Uses and Disclosures

**Policy**

Individuals may request to restrict certain uses or disclosures of their PHI:
- Each Covered Unit will allow an individual to request that the Covered Unit restrict its use or disclosure of the individual's PHI for the purposes of treatment, payment, or health care operations, or to family or others involved in the individual's care including disaster relief organizations.
- A Covered Unit is not required to agree to an individual's request, with one exception: when an individual has paid in full for an item or service (self-pay), the Covered Unit must agree to the individual's request to not disclose PHI related to that item or service to the health plan.
- The Covered Unit will not use or disclose PHI in violation of an agreed-upon restriction, except in medical emergencies when the restricted PHI is needed to provide emergency treatment.

Individuals may request to be contacted in a confidential manner. A reasonable request must be in writing, specify how payment will be handled if the request involves a change in billing procedures, and provide a specific alternative address and/or adequate details describing the alternative means of communication.

Reference: 45 CFR §§ 164.502(c), 164.522(a) and (b)

### 8.6 Right to File a Complaint

**Policy**

The City HIPAA Privacy Officer (CHPO) is the City-designated contact to receive complaints from individuals regarding the HIPAA compliance of any Covered Unit, Support Unit, or City workforce member, or relating to any City or unit-specific HIPAA policy, procedures, or protocol.

The CHPO will maintain documentation of any such individual complaint, including its investigation and disposition, for at least six years following the resolution of such complaint.

The City will cooperate with any audits or investigations by DHHS relating to the City's HIPAA compliance.

Reference: 45 CFR §§ 164.530(d), 160.306

## 9.0 Administration and Training

### 9.1 HIPAA Officials

**Policy**

Executive Order No. 4-17: HIPAA Compliance Directive establishes an executive-level HIPAA Steering Committee and designates the City HIPAA Privacy Officer (CHPO) and City HIPAA Security Officer (CHSO) as the officials with overall responsibility for City-level HIPAA compliance and administration.

The CHPO is responsible for: monitoring the City's HIPAA compliance; approving unit-specific HIPAA policies, procedures, and protocols; coordinating the investigation and reporting of HIPAA incidents; managing the City's response to governmental audits and investigations; and providing guidance to City departments and offices.

The CHSO is responsible for overseeing all activities related to the development, implementation, and management of adherence to the City's policies and procedures ensuring the security, availability, and integrity of electronic PHI.

Each Covered and Support Unit will designate a Privacy Officer/Liaison to oversee HIPAA compliance and administration within that unit and serve as the unit's liaison with the CHPO.

### 9.2 Training and Education

**Policy**

Covered Units are responsible for ensuring that all their workforce members receive HIPAA training. HIPAA training includes: (a) the required City HIPAA training module; and (b) training specific to the activities and HIPAA protocols of the Covered or Support Unit.

HIPAA training will be provided to: (a) all new workforce members no later than 30 days after their start date; (b) all current workforce members affected by a significant change to City or unit-specific policies and procedures, within 45 days of the change becoming effective; and (c) on a periodic basis as required to refresh workforce members' knowledge, preferably annually.

Training records will be maintained for six years and provided to the CHPO upon request.

Reference: 45 CFR § 164.530(b)

### 9.3 Sanctions

**Policy**

City workforce members will be subject to disciplinary action for violation of City or unit-specific HIPAA policies and procedures. Violations that jeopardize the privacy or security of PHI are particularly serious and will subject the responsible workforce member(s) to disciplinary action up to and including termination of employment.

**Table 9.3: Sample Disciplinary Actions Resulting from HIPAA Violations**

| Class | Examples of Violations | Examples of Disciplinary Actions |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Accessing information without need; sharing computer access codes; leaving computer unattended while logged into a PHI program; sharing PHI with another employee without authorization; copying or changing PHI without authorization; discussing confidential information in public areas | Verbal reprimand; Written reprimand; Retraining on HIPAA awareness; Retraining on City privacy and security policies; Retraining on proper use of internal and HIPAA required forms |
| 2 | Second offense of any class 1 offense; unauthorized use or disclosure of PHI; using another person's computer access codes; failure to comply with a Privacy Officer resolution | Written reprimand; Retraining (all forms listed above); Suspension of employee (recommended: 1-3 days) |
| 3 | Third offense of any class 1 offense; second offense of any class 2 offense; obtaining PHI under false pretenses; using and/or disclosing PHI for commercial advantage, personal gain or malicious harm | Termination of employment; Civil penalties as provided under HIPAA or other applicable law; Criminal penalties as provided under HIPAA or other applicable law |

Reference: 45 CFR §§ 164.530(e), 164.308(1)

### 9.4 Anti-Retaliation

**Policy**

The City prohibits any intimidating or retaliatory acts be taken against any individual or other person (including a workforce member) for:
- Exercising their rights or participating in any process established by the HIPAA Rules, such as filing a complaint with DHHS.
- Testifying, assisting, or participating in an investigation, compliance review, proceeding, or hearing authorized by the HIPAA Rules.
- Opposing any act or practice that violates the HIPAA Rules, provided the person has a good faith belief that the practice opposed is unlawful and the manner of opposition is reasonable.

Workforce members who are found to have committed intimidating or retaliatory acts will be subject to sanctions under Section 9.3, up to and including termination.

Reference: 45 CFR §§ 160.316, 164.530(g)

### 9.5 HIPAA Documentation

**Policy**

HIPAA Documentation will be retained for a minimum of six years from the date of creation or the date when last in effect, whichever is later.

HIPAA Documentation includes:
- City HIPAA policies and procedures
- Unit-specific and departmental HIPAA policies, procedures, or protocols
- Any communication required by the HIPAA Rules
- Any action, activity, or designation required to be documented by the HIPAA Rules
- Incident documentation for any privacy and security incidents, and breach notification documentation for any Breaches
- Business associate agreements with service providers and contractors

Reference: 45 CFR § 164.530(j)(1)
