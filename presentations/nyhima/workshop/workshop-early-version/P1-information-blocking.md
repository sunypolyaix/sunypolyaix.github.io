## situation

Your compliance team has asked you to help staff understand a key provision of the ONC information blocking rule. You need to read the regulatory language and turn it into something practical.

## source

Excerpt — 45 CFR Part 171 (ONC Information Blocking Rule)

"An actor engages in information blocking if the actor knows, or should know, that the practice is likely to interfere with, prevent, or materially discourage access, exchange, or use of electronic health information, unless the actor is a health care provider and the practice is required by law or covered by an exception."

Exceptions (summary):
  - Preventing Harm Exception: Practice is reasonable and necessary to prevent patient harm
  - Privacy Exception: Practice is consistent with applicable privacy laws
  - Security Exception: Practice is necessary to protect security of EHI
  - Infeasibility Exception: Fulfilling the request is infeasible
  - Health IT Performance Exception: Necessary to maintain or improve system performance
  - Content and Manner Exception: Actor responds in a technically different but permitted manner
  - Fees Exception: Charges are reasonable and cost-based
  - Licensing Exception: Relates to licensing of interoperability elements

Penalties: Up to $1,000,000 per violation for health IT developers and health information networks; disincentives for health care providers under separate rulemaking.
