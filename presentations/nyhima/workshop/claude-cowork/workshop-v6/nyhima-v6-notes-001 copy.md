## NOTE 1 — Round 1 lesson line: three framings, not one

**What v5 says:**
Slide 12 (Round 1 Debrief) carries a facilitator note: *"the exact Round-1 lesson
line is still yours to lock — you set aside 'ask the data / curiosity.' The working
frame here is 'the questions are yours.' Swap in the final wording when you have it."*

**What this session resolved:**
The single-frame lesson line was resisted for a good reason: any one frame either
overclaims the participant's role or the model's. Writing all three framings into
the debrief is the right move. The facilitator reads the room first, then closes
with the frame that fits what actually happened at the tables.

**Three framings, with their conditions:**

| If this happened at the tables… | Close with… |
|---|---|
| Participants found what they expected — confirmed a hunch, felt smart | *"Ask without a thesis. See what the data says back."* |
| Model surfaced something they weren't looking for — a pattern they didn't anticipate | *"The model doesn't know what matters. Neither did you yet — and that was the right starting point."* |
| Debate at the tables about what the finding means, or who produced it | *"You brought the question. The model brought a pattern. What you do with that is the work."* |

**The frame behind the framings — grounded theory:**
The model is a good instrument for open inquiry because it doesn't bring a thesis
to the data either. What we used to call grounded theory: go in without a hypothesis,
let categories emerge, follow the thread the data opens. Round 1 should encourage
this — not just confirm. The lesson line should never imply the participant always
finds the story. The model might find it. That's fine. That's good.

**Action for v6:**
Replace the bracketed facilitator note in Slide 12 with the three-framing structure
above. Add a brief preamble: *"Read the room before you close. Use the frame that
fits what you saw."*

## NOTE 2 — Round 3 language: "put your name on it"

**What v5 says:**
- Slide 2 (Opening): *"iterate on something until it sounds like you wrote it"*
- Slide 7 (Three Moves table): *"Until it sounds like you own it."*
- Slide 18 (Round 3 Debrief): *"You iterated until it sounded like you. That's the
  move that makes something worth your name."* — this one is already correct.

**What this session resolved:**
*"Until it sounds like you wrote it"* is the wrong frame. The goal is not sonic
resemblance to the participant's voice — it's ownership and accountability. The LP
(Phase III, Exercise 11 forward) makes this explicit: every artifact carries full
citation — name, date, model, transcript link. The citation is not a formality.
It's what it means to put your name on something.

The replacement: **"until it reads like something you'll put your name on."**

**Sources in the LP:**
- Phase III citation block (Exercises 11–15): *"Generated with AI assistance.
  Student: [name] | Date: [date] | Model: [model name] | Transcript: [link]"*
- The Prompter's Note (Exercise 15): *"what you tried, what didn't work, what
  you'd do differently"* — this is the posture of someone who owns the work.
- Exercise 14 ("Go Public"): can the synthesis document support a public-facing
  piece of writing without the model reaching beyond it? If yes, the synthesis —
  and by extension the artifact — is something you'd stand behind.

**Note on Exercise 13:**
Steve remembered "Did You Learn Your Own Slop?" — that's Exercise 13, not 14.
Exercise 14 is "Go Public." Both are relevant to the Round 3 frame but 13 is
the sharper provocation for the debrief question *"Would you put your name on
the final version?"* — did you generate without learning? That question belongs
in Slide 18.

**Action for v6:**
- Slide 2: *"iterate on something until it sounds like you wrote it"* →
  *"iterate on something until it reads like something you'll put your name on"*
- Slide 7 table: *"Until it sounds like you own it."* →
  *"Until it reads like something you'll put your name on."*
- Slide 18 facilitator note: replace the bracketed placeholder with a note
  pointing to LP Exercise 13 ("Did You Learn Your Own Slop?") as the source of
  the debrief question and LP Phase III citation discipline as the conceptual anchor.

## NOTE 4 — Tone flag for v6 writing

From the June 6 session, a standing principle for any v6 language that describes
what participants do with AI output:

The frame is **agency, not adaptation.** Not *"get used to it"* — *"have agency
over it."* This applies to verb choices, debrief language, and any framing of the
workshop's argument. The opening question *"Who prompted that?"* sets this up.
Every lesson line should sustain it.

The weight of what we are asking participants to do — exercise judgment over systems
that moved fast and arrived before most professional training caught up — deserves
acknowledgment, not cheerleading. Participants work in healthcare. The stakes of
automation bias in their context are not abstract.

## NOTE 5 — workshop.html: updates needed for v6

Note 3 above logs the four fixes already applied on June 6. This note covers
what workshop.html should carry when v6 is built. The public page and the
facilitator deck are separate artifacts but they describe the same workshop —
when the deck changes, the page needs to stay in sync.

**5a — Round 1 verb card: reflect the grounded theory frame**

Current (post-June 6 fix):
*"CMS hospital dataset filtered to New York. Ask questions. Surface patterns.
The questions are yours — the model helps you see the numbers."*

Problem: *"The questions are yours"* is directional but too triumphalist.
It implies the participant always drives the finding. The grounded theory
frame says: go in without a hypothesis, the model might surface something
you didn't expect, and that's the right outcome — not a failure of the method.

Proposed v6 replacement:
*"CMS hospital dataset filtered to New York. Ask open questions. Let the
model surface patterns. The interesting result might be one you weren't
looking for."*

Or, if a tighter version is needed:
*"CMS hospital dataset filtered to New York. Ask without a thesis.
The model reads faster than you — the question is whether you can
follow where the data leads."*

Decision deferred to after June 7. Workshop experience will sharpen this.

**5b — Round 1 run-of-show row**

Current:
*"CMS hospital data, New York. Read → think → write. Workbench prompt at the end."*

This is clean and accurate. No change needed for v6 unless the Round 1
lesson language hardens into something worth surfacing in the run-of-show.
Hold for now.

**5c — Round 3 verb card: already correct**

The June 6 fix brought this into alignment: *"until it reads like something
you'll put your name on."* No further change needed here for v6 unless the
LP connection (Phase III citation discipline) warrants a different phrasing
after the workshop.

**5d — Round 2 verb card: already correct**

The June 6 fix dropped the second-model framing. Current text:
*"Feed it to the model, get an artifact back. Read what the model made of it.
Decide what it missed — and what only your expertise can supply."*

The *"only your expertise can supply"* clause is load-bearing — it places
professional judgment as the thing the model cannot substitute. Worth keeping
verbatim in v6. The deck's Slide 14 (Round 2: How It Works) should carry
the same clause for consistency.

**5e — Three Operations: consider adding a sub-label register**

Currently each verb card has a `verb-sub` label:
- Round 1: *"Working with data"*
- Round 2: *"Working with text"*
- Round 3: *"Working with your own draft"*

These are accurate but flat. In v6, consider whether the sub-labels should
carry more of the cognitive move — e.g.:
- Round 1: *"Open inquiry"*
- Round 2: *"Critical reading"*
- Round 3: *"Accountable revision"*

Decision deferred. These labels appear on the public page and should be
reviewed against what participants actually say they experienced in Round 1–3.
Post-workshop observation will settle this.

**5f — Overview paragraph: consider one sentence on human stakes**

Current second paragraph:
*"The workshop is built around three cognitive operations — Interrogate,
Interpret, Iterate — each applied to a different artifact type. Inside every
round, participants read, think, and write. The round name marks the dominant move."*

This is accurate. For v6 consider whether the overview should carry one
sentence that names the stakes — not the efficiency argument, but the
accountability argument. Something like:

*"HIM professionals work with systems that make recommendations before the
professional has formed a view. These three operations are practice for the
kind of judgment that has always mattered — and matters more now."*

This would be consistent with the agency/adaptation tone principle in Note 4.
Decision deferred — this is also the argument of the June 9 talk, and the
two should be written together rather than separately.
