## intro (2 min)

"First bucket: Data. These scenarios give you a table or a set of numbers—the kind of thing that lands in your inbox with a question attached. Your job is to read it with a model, then have a second model check the first one's work."

"Pick any scenario in the D bucket. If your table wants to split up and do different ones, go ahead. You have 25 minutes."

Point to the Drive folder: **scenarios/data/**

## the sprint cycle — post this or keep it on screen

```
1. Read your scenario situation
2. Paste the source document into Model A
3. Run the writing prompt
4. Save or copy the output
5. Open Model B (different model)
6. Paste the validation prompt
7. Paste in Model A's output
8. Compare
9. Answer the reflection question — just think it, don't write it yet
```

## what you do during the sprint

Circulate. Listen for:
- Someone surprised by what the second model caught
- Someone frustrated that both models said the same thing
- Someone who noticed the model was confidently wrong

Those are your debrief voices. Note who they are.

Ask tables: "Which model are you on?" Nudge anyone who hasn't switched models yet.

## debrief (10 min)

Open with: **"Did the second model catch something the first one missed?"**

Take 3–4 responses. Then push:

- "Did anyone have both models agree—and then realize they were both wrong?"
- "Where did your own knowledge have to step in?"

Land the lesson: **Two models are better than one. But two models plus your expertise is better than either.**

Don't over-explain. Let the room's experience carry it. Move to Sprint 2.

## if tables finish early

Point them to the next D scenario. Or ask: "Try the same scenario with a different system prompt. Does the output change?"

## notes

The data scenarios are the most accessible—good place to build confidence before Policy and Judgment raise the stakes. If the room is slow to start, do a quick demo of D1 on the shared screen: paste the source, run the writing prompt, show the output. 90 seconds. Then hand it back to them.
