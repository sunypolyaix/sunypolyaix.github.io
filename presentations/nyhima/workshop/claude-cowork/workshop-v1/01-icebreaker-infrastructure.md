## purpose

Get everyone into a model. Surface platform variance immediately. Establish the Drive folder as home base. Seed the "two models are better than one" lesson before the first real sprint.

## step 1: get them connected (5 min)

Show the QR code or say the URL: **tiny.cc/nyhima**

"Open that on your laptop or tablet. Everything you need today lives there—scenarios, system prompts, instructions. You don't need to install anything. You just need a browser and access to one of these: ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini. If you have all three, even better."

Circulate. Table captains help neighbors. Don't wait for 100%—start the icebreaker when most tables are ready.

## step 2: the icebreaker prompt (8 min)

"Before we do anything else, I want you to try something simple. Open your model. Paste this prompt exactly as written."

**ICEBREAKER PROMPT** (also in Drive as system-prompts/00-icebreaker.txt):

> You are an expert in health information management. In exactly one sentence, describe what HIM professionals do—written for a hospital board member who has never heard the term.

"Run it. Read what you get. Now—if you have a second model open, run it there too."

Wait 2–3 minutes. Then ask the room:

- "What did your model say?"
- "Did anyone get something surprising?"
- "Did two models give you different sentences?"

Take 3–4 responses. Point out the variance. That's the first lesson: **same prompt, different models, different outputs.** The model is a variable, not a constant.

## step 3: introduce the system prompt library (5 min)

"In the Drive folder you'll find a folder called system-prompts. These are pre-written instructions that change how the model behaves before you ask your real question. Think of them as lenses. We'll use them throughout the day."

"System prompt 1 makes the model a skeptical reviewer. System prompt 2 makes it a plain-language translator. System prompt 3 makes it argue the other side. We'll talk about which one to use when."

Demo on the shared screen: open system-prompt-01.txt, copy it, paste it into a model as the first message, then ask a follow-up. Show that the model's behavior changes.

## step 4: orient to the day (2 min)

"We have three buckets of scenarios—Data, Policy, and Judgment. Each table will work through one bucket at a time together. You pick your scenario within the bucket. You can do one or four—your call. After each bucket we debrief as a room."

"At the end of the day, you have whatever you saved. That's your workbench. It's yours."

## notes

The icebreaker prompt is deliberately easy. The point isn't the answer—it's the variance. If two models at the same table give meaningfully different sentences, that's worth more than anything you could say about model differences.

If someone doesn't have access to any model, pair them with someone who does. Don't spend more than 2 minutes solving individual tech problems—keep moving.
