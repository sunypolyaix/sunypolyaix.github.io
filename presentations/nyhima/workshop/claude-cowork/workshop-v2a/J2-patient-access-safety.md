## situation

A patient submits a formal request for their complete medical record under HIPAA's right of access. The record includes psychiatric notes from a recent inpatient stay following a suicide attempt. The treating psychiatrist has contacted you directly and said: "I believe releasing these notes to this patient right now poses a serious risk of harm. I'm asking you to withhold them."

## source

HIPAA Right of Access (45 CFR § 164.524):

Individuals have the right to access their own PHI in a designated record set.

Permitted grounds for denial (reviewable):
- A licensed health care professional has determined that access is reasonably likely to endanger the life or physical safety of the individual or another person.
- The information was compiled in reasonable anticipation of litigation.

If denial is based on endangerment, the individual has the right to have the denial reviewed by a licensed health care professional designated by the covered entity who did not participate in the original denial decision.

The covered entity must provide the individual with a written denial that includes the basis, the individual's right to request a review, and how to file a complaint.

Timeframe: 30 days to respond; 30-day extension possible with written notice.
