## situation

Your hospital has piloted an ambient AI documentation tool that listens to patient encounters and generates clinical notes. Physicians review and sign the notes. A physician comes to you with a concern: she reviewed a note the AI generated, made minor edits, and signed it—but she realizes now the note contains a clinical inference she never made and doesn't actually agree with. The note is already in the record and has been used for a billing submission.

## source

AHIMA Position Statement on Clinical Documentation Integrity (excerpt, paraphrased for workshop use):

Clinical documentation must accurately reflect the care provided. The author of record bears responsibility for the content of any note they sign, regardless of how it was generated. Health information professionals have a responsibility to support the integrity of the health record and to flag discrepancies that could affect patient safety, billing accuracy, or legal liability.

There is currently no universal standard for disclosure of AI-generated content in clinical documentation. Some health systems require a notation; others do not. CMS has not issued final guidance on AI-generated clinical notes and coding.

The False Claims Act imposes liability for knowingly submitting false claims to federal payers. "Knowingly" includes reckless disregard for the truth.
