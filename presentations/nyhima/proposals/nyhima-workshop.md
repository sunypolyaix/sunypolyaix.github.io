## Workshop Synopsis

You'll learn to use large language models to "read" by uploading real information and having the model help you interpret it. And to "write" by using the cycle of prompting, reading generated responses, and integrating them into your own thinking—generating texts, charts, or graphs to reflect your knowledge in a way you can share. Hands-on practice with actual data. Participants understand when and how these practices might serve their professional work.

## Extended Workshop Synopsis

Most people use large language models like ChatGPT, Gemini, or Claude by prompting for generated responses—then simply consuming those responses. They treat the model as an output generator: compose a request, get results, use them or discard them. This workshop teaches something fundamentally different.

The workshop is grounded in two core practices: **reading with a large language model** and **writing with a large language model**. These are distinct from prompting for outputs.

### Reading with a Large Language Model

You have information or data you need to understand—complex documentation, large datasets, organizational information. Instead of trying to comprehend it alone, you upload it, feed it into a large language model, and use the model as an interpretive partner. You formulate questions that help the model understand what you need to interpret. You work iteratively with the model's responses, deepening your comprehension. You evaluate when the model's interpretation helps and when your own expertise must prevail. Through this cycle of uploading, questioning, and integrating, you understand something complex with the model's help.

### Writing with a Large Language Model

You have knowledge developed through your reasoning or analysis that you want to extend, articulate, and share with others. Rather than working alone, you write that thinking as prompts to a large language model—not polished or final, but genuine attempts to articulate what you're working through. The model generates responses. You read those responses, review them, and integrate them into your thinking—or use them as your thinking. You write more prompts based on what emerges. Through this cycle of writing, reading generated responses, and integrating them, your thinking becomes clearer and more precise. You generate texts, charts, or graphs that reflect your knowledge in a way you can share with others.

Both practices require skill, judgment, and intention. Both are different from simply requesting outputs and consuming them. Both are grounded in your expertise and your professional judgment.

Hands-on practice with actual HIM data allows participants to experience both practices directly. You'll use a large language model to read real information and to develop real thinking you care about. Through that experience, participants understand when and how these practices might serve their professional work—and when they won't.
