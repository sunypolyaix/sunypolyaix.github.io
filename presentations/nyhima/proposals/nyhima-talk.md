## Presentation Synopsis

This talk argues that AI literacy should be understood as a core professional competency for Health Information Management, not as an optional technical skill.

Large language models increasingly participate in reading and writing work across healthcare organizations. They interpret documents, summarize records, draft guidance, and shape workflows. As this participation grows, HIM professionals need literacy with these systems: the ability to read with them, write with them, and think critically about them.

AI literacy means three things: reading with large language models to interpret complex information. Writing with them to develop and articulate your professional reasoning. And critiquing them—understanding what they actually are. AI is not magic or cloud-based intelligence. It runs on material infrastructure: data centers that consume energy, minerals extracted from the ground, labor systems. Understanding this material reality is a prerequisite to using AI responsibly.

Everyone should have access to AI. Not because it solves problems, but because literacy with these tools is a component of professional judgment in fields like HIM. But that access only matters if it comes with understanding: understanding what AI actually is, what it can and cannot do, and what the ethical implications are for healthcare, for data, for people.

For HIM professionals, this means treating AI literacy as a workplace expectation: supported through continuing education, embedded in professional standards, and considered in hiring and advancement decisions. This session offers a framework for why that matters and how your field can embrace it.
