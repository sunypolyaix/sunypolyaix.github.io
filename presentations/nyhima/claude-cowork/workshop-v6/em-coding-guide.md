# Evaluation and Management (E/M) Coding Guide

**Source:** CareOregon · Last revised: March 2026

---

## Purpose

This policy addresses evaluation and management (E/M) services and promotes correct coding and billing practices. The goal is to ensure consistent and appropriate selection of E/M codes throughout the network and keep medical costs consistent.

The E/M coding section of the CPT® book is divided into broad categories with further subcategories describing various E/M service classifications. The classification of the E/M service is important because the nature of the work varies by type and place of service, the patient's medical status, and other code criteria, along with the amount of the provider's work and documentation required. Key components appear in the descriptors for most basic E/M codes; many code categories describe increasing levels of complexity.

Providers are encouraged to consult the most current CPT® Professional Edition published by the American Medical Association.

---

## Evaluation and Management Overview

E/M guidelines cover the following service categories:

| Category | Code Range |
|---|---|
| Office or Other Outpatient Services | 99202–99215 |
| Telemedicine Services | 98000–98016 |
| Hospital Inpatient and Observation Care Services | 99221–99239 |
| Consultations | 99242–99255 |
| Emergency Department Services | 99281–99288 |
| Nursing Facility Services | 99304–99316 |
| Home or Residence Services | 99341–99350 |
| Prolonged Service (with or without direct patient contact) | 99358–99360 |

The E/M section (98000–98016, 99202–99499) includes many code categories that may have specific guidelines, or codes may include specific details. The E/M guidelines in this guide apply to the categories listed above.

Most broad categories are further divided into subcategories (e.g., new patient and established patient; initial and subsequent). The basic format of E/M services with levels is based on **medical decision making (MDM) or time**. Place of service (POS) and service type are defined by the location where the encounter with the patient and/or family/caregiver occurs.

---

## New and Established Patients

**New patient:** A patient who has not received any professional services from the physician or qualified health care professional (QHP) of the same specialty and subspecialty who belongs to the same group practice within the **past three years**.

**Established patient:** A patient who has received professional services from the physician/QHP or another physician/QHP of the same specialty and subspecialty who belongs to the same group practice within the **past three years**.

**Additional rules:**
- In an FQHC setting, the new vs. established patient distinction differs. See Chapter 13 of the Medicare Claims Processing Manual (70.3) New Patient Adjustment.
- When a physician/QHP is on call or covering for another, the patient's encounter is classified as it would have been by the unavailable physician/QHP.
- Advanced practice nurses and physician assistants working with physicians are considered to be working in the same specialty and subspecialty as the physician.
- **No new vs. established patient distinction is made in the emergency department.**

---

## Initial and Subsequent Services (Inpatient and Observation Care)

Some categories do not distinguish between new and established patients (e.g., hospital inpatient and observation care). These services are differentiated by **initial** or **subsequent** service instead.

**Initial service:** The patient has not received professional service(s) from the physician/QHP or another physician/QHP of the exact same specialty and subspecialty who belongs to the same group practice during the admission and stay.

**Subsequent service:** The patient has received professional service(s) from the physician/QHP or another physician/QHP of the exact same specialty and subspecialty who belongs to the same group practice during the admission and stay.

When a physician/QHP is on call or covering for another, the encounter is classified as it would have been by the unavailable physician/QHP. Advanced practice nurses and physician assistants working with physicians are considered to be working in the same specialty or subspecialty as the physician.

---

## Split or Shared Visits

Physicians/QHPs may work together during a single E/M service. The service should be reported by the professional who spent a majority of the face-to-face or non-face-to-face time performing the service, provided that professional performed a **substantive portion** of the encounter.

E/M services based on MDM require that the physician/QHP who made or approved the management plan — considering the number and complexity of problems addressed — takes responsibility for the plan, including the risk of complications and/or morbidity/mortality of patient management.

---

## Services Reported Separately

Any identifiable procedure or service (identified with a CPT code) on the same date of service as an E/M may be reported separately.

The ordering, performance, and/or interpretation of a diagnostic test is **not included** in determining the level of an E/M service when the professional interpretation of the test is reported separately.

---

## Policy

CareOregon must comply with industry-standard coding guidelines. All reported services must be supported by the medical record. CareOregon conducts periodic analysis of current and historical claim data and may request medical records to ensure accurate and appropriate reimbursement for E/M services that are frequently miscoded and upcoded.

Codes reported by physicians or other health care professionals should best represent the services provided based on AMA and CMS documentation guidelines.

**If the E/M code submitted is higher than the level supported by the medical record, CareOregon may:**
- Adjust reimbursement to reflect the lower level of E/M
- Deny the claim (providers have the right to appeal via the dispute resolution process)
- Recover payments previously made on claims that exceed the appropriate E/M level

Code revision dates:
- Office and Outpatient E/M (99202–99205, 99211–99215): effective January 1, 2021
- Non-Office E/M, Consultations, ED Services, Nursing Facility, Home/Residence Services: revised January 1, 2023

## Selecting the Level of Service (LOS) Based on Time

Except for CPT codes 99281–99285, providers may choose the appropriate E/M level based on either time or MDM. **CPT codes 99281–99285 (Emergency Department) use only MDM.**

**Time documentation criteria — included:**
- Examination and evaluation
- Counseling and education
- Prep time for patient history and test reviews
- Documentation and interpretation
- Care coordination, referrals, and communication with other providers
- Orders for tests, procedures, and medication

**Time documentation criteria — not included:**
- Time spent by clinical staff
- Patient wait time for the physician or other provider
- Additional distinct service procedures provided on the same date as the E/M service

---

## Selecting the Level of Service Based on Medical Decision Making (MDM)

MDM is determined by **two of three elements** meeting or exceeding the threshold for a given level:

1. **Number and complexity of problem(s) addressed**

2. **Amount and/or complexity of data reviewed and analyzed**
   - Orders for, and interpretation of, data from a test or image cannot be included when determining E/M level if the test or image interpretation is billed separately.

3. **Risk of complications and/or morbidity or mortality of patient management**
   - CareOregon uses CMS Manual System Pub 100 definitions: codes with "090" in Field 16 are major surgeries; codes with "000" or "010" are minor surgical procedures or endoscopies.

When determining the level of MDM, **two of the three elements for that level must be met or exceeded.**

---

## Emergency Department Visits — Patient Status

Time is **not** a descriptive component for emergency department E/M levels of service.

Providers must use CPT codes 99281–99285 for emergency department visits for both established and new patients. Providers requested to serve as a consult should use the appropriate consultation E/M code.

CPT codes 99281–99285 must only be submitted for services provided in an emergency department defined as: *"an organized hospital-based facility for the provision of unscheduled episodic services to patients who present for immediate medical attention. The facility must be available 24 hours a day."* This definition includes hospital-owned free-standing emergency departments.

## Definitions

**Clinical staff:** Staff working under the supervision of a physician or QHP who may assist in performing a specified professional service but do not independently bill for that service. Includes medical assistants, LPNs, RNs, and similar roles.

**Encounter:** Interaction between a covered member and a health care provider for which E/M or other services are rendered and results in a claim submission.

**Evaluation and management:** E/M codes represent services by a physician or other health care professional in which the provider is evaluating or managing a patient's health. Diagnostic tests, radiology, surgery, and other therapies are not considered E/M services.

**FQHC:** Federally Qualified Health Center.

**Level of service (LOS):** The complexity and extent of E/M services provided to a patient during a clinical encounter.

**Medical decision making (MDM):** Reflects the complexity of the cognitive work performed by the provider during a patient encounter — how difficult it was to assess, diagnose, and manage the patient's condition based on standardized criteria.

**Qualified Health Care Professional (QHP):** An individual qualified by training, education, licensure, and/or regulation who performs a professional service within their scope of practice and independently reports that service. Examples: NP, PA, CNS, CNM, CSW, PT.

**Upcoding:** Submitting codes for more severe conditions than diagnosed in order to receive higher reimbursement.
