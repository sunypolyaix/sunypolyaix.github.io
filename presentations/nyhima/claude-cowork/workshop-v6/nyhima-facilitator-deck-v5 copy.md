# Reading and Writing with AI
## Facilitator Deck — v5
### NYHIMA Workshop | Sunday, June 7, 2026 | 1:00–4:00 PM | Steve Schneider, SUNY Poly AIX Center

The workshop runs a guided **lightning round** and then three self-directed rounds:
**Interrogate → Interpret → Iterate.** Each round is one cognitive move on a real
HIM artifact. Inside every round, participants still **read, think, and write** —
the round is named for the move that carries it.

The opening question — **"Who prompted that?"** — and the closing line —
**"It is worth exactly what you put into it"** — are the bookends.

Base path is dead simple: one model, one artifact, share one link. Everything
richer (a second model, the encores) is optional, for tables that are ahead.
When in doubt, keep the room on the simple path.

# SLIDE 2 — OPENING
*(10 min · start without slides — just talk)*

> ## Who prompted that?

**What you say:**

"Before anything else — one question. Think about the last time AI showed up in
your work. Not something you went looking for. Something already there. A
suggested code. A flagged record. A pre-populated field.

*Who prompted that?*

Most of what gets called AI literacy assumes *you're* the one asking. You type,
it answers, you decide. That's one kind of AI. It's not the only kind — and for
a lot of you, it may not even be the main kind.

Today we work with the kind you ask. We'll do it three ways: we'll **interrogate**
data, **interpret** a document, and **iterate** on something until it sounds like
you wrote it. By 4:00 you'll have things you made and can take back to work."

**What you don't say:**
- Don't explain what a language model is. They'll see it.
- Don't apologize for the tech. If it breaks, that's data.
- Don't promise it'll be impressive. It won't always be — that's the lesson.

# SLIDE 6 — WHAT YOU JUST SAW

How you introduce **yourself** to the model changes what you get back.

- "**I am** an HIM professional" → it talks *to* a peer.
- "**You are** an HIM professional" → it performs a costume.

> You're the expert in the room. Make the model adjust to you —
> not the other way around.

The model is a variable, not a constant. Hold onto that.

# SLIDE 7 — THE THREE MOVES
*(standalone — the spine of the day)*

> ## Interrogate → Interpret → Iterate

| Move | You work with | The real work |
|------|---------------|---------------|
| **Interrogate** | Data | Ask it questions. Surface patterns. *The questions are yours.* |
| **Interpret** | Dense text | Make sense of it. Catch what's soft, missing, or wrong. *Your expertise decides.* |
| **Iterate** | Your own draft | Push back, refine, again. *Until it sounds like you own it.* |

Inside each one you'll still **read, think, and write**. The name tells you
which move is doing the heavy lifting.

# SLIDE 11 — ROUND 1: HOW IT WORKS
*(leave on screen during the round)*

**Interrogate — read, think, write with data**

```
1. READ   Upload the data. "Summarize the structure. What's in here?"
2. THINK  Pick a story it could tell. "I want to compare ___ to ___."
3. WRITE  "Make a one-page memo with a chart that tells that story to leadership."
4. SAVE   Run the workbench prompt. Share your Gemini link in your row.
```

*Hit a wall on file size or tokens? Start a fresh Gemini conversation.*
*Ahead of the room? Ask it something it would get wrong — and catch it.*

# SLIDE 13 — ROUND 2: INTERPRET
*(30 min · self-directed · text)*

> ## Interpret the text

**Model:** Gemini (free).
**Document:** pick one from **tiny.cc/nyhima → documents** — a real, dense HIM document.

**What you say:**

"Paste the document in. Ask it to make the thing plain. Then read its answer like
a skeptic — what did it soften? what did it skip? what would trip you up if you
hadn't read the original? You're not taking dictation. You're interpreting,
with help."

**You have 25 minutes. Go.**

---

# SLIDE 14 — ROUND 2: HOW IT WORKS
*(leave on screen during the round)*

**Interpret — read, think, write with text**

```
1. READ   Paste the document. "Put this in plain language for clinical staff."
2. THINK  Where are the traps? "What does this miss? What's legally soft?"
3. WRITE  Produce the artifact — guidance, checklist, or memo.
4. SAVE   Run the workbench prompt. Share your Gemini link in your row.
```

*Ahead of the room? Open a second model, paste your artifact, ask:*
*"A colleague wrote this. What did they miss?" — see what a second reader catches.*

# SLIDE 15 — ROUND 2: DEBRIEF

**A model that sounds authoritative about the law is a specific kind of dangerous.**

- If you didn't already know this regulation, how would you know it got it wrong?
- Would you hand the model's version to a colleague without editing it?

> One reader misses things. Your expertise is what makes the interpretation
> trustworthy.

---

# SLIDE 16 — ROUND 3: ITERATE
*(25 min · self-directed · tone)*

> ## Iterate until it's yours

**What you say:**

"Take something you made today — your memo, your guidance. Paste it back in. Now
we're not fixing facts. We're fixing *voice*. Ask: does this sound like I made a
decision, or like I'm hedging? Too soft? Too alarming? Push back. Run it again.
Then again. Stop when it sounds like you."

**You have 20 minutes. Go.**

---

# SLIDE 17 — ROUND 3: HOW IT WORKS
*(leave on screen during the round)*

**Iterate — read, think, write on your own draft**

```
1. READ   Paste your own artifact back in.
2. THINK  "Does this sound like I own it, or like I'm hedging? What's the tone?"
3. WRITE  "Make it sound more like a decision." Run it. Push back. Again.
4. SAVE   Run the workbench prompt. Share your Gemini link in your row.
```

*The back-and-forth is the point. One pass is not iteration.*

# SLIDE 19 — IF YOU'RE AHEAD: TWO ENCORES
*(only for tables that finish early — not required)*

**Encore A — Judgment.** Pick a scenario in **tiny.cc/nyhima → judgment** (J1–J4).
*Write your position first — what should happen and why — before you open the model.*
Then make the model argue against you. Watch it perform balance when you needed a call.

**Encore B — The Documentation Gap.** Bring **both** a data file and a policy
together. Interrogate the data (where are we failing?), interpret the policy (what
does it require?), iterate toward a one-page memo to your CEO that names the gap
and recommends three actions.

> Real work is never just data or just text. It's both — and it takes all three
> moves to get to something worth acting on.

# SLIDE 20 — WRAP: THE META-MOMENT
*(15 min)*

**What you do:**

"Everyone got their link into the shared doc? Watch this."

Pull the shared links, compile them on the projector, and show the room their own
work, side by side.

> ## AI just read everything you produced today.

**What you say:**

"Here's what thirty of you made in three hours. Same data, different stories.
Same document, different reads. I just used AI to read all of your AI work — in
about thirty seconds.

So — who gets credit for this?

The model interrogated, interpreted, helped you iterate. *You* decided which
questions mattered, what the document actually meant, and when the tone was
right. The model did none of that. You did."

*(The Morrisville frame: "AI did the work — can I get credit for it?" Let the
question sit. Don't answer it. They already know.)*

# SLIDE 21 — CLOSE

> ## It is worth exactly what you put into it.

A vague prompt gets a vague answer. A document you didn't read gets a summary you
can't trust. A real question, real expertise, and the patience to iterate — that
gets you something you can use.

You proved it three times today. You're holding the proof.

The AI already in your workflows — the kind that flags and suggests before you've
formed a view — that one's harder to interrogate. But now you have a practice for
it. You know what to ask. You know "the AI said so" isn't an answer.
