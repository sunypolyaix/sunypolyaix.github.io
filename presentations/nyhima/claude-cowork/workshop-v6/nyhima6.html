Round 1 · 30 min · self-directed · Gemini free

Interrogate the data — pick one file

FileWhat's in itPick this if you want…
Complications & Deaths 30-day mortality, hip/knee, pressure ulcers, PSI-90 — 20 measures …the richest storytelling. One NY surgical-mortality number lands at 240. First question: "what's the denominator?"
Hospital Readmissions (HRRP) Excess Readmission Ratio for six conditions. ERR > 1.0 = Medicare penalty exposure …a clean memo in fifteen minutes. Sort descending, write the penalty-risk memo. Do you push back on what the ERR actually measures?
Patient Safety (PSI-6) AHRQ adverse-event rates at six-decimal precision — iatrogenic pneumothorax, PSI-90 composite …to see precision masquerade as meaning. PSI-06 doesn't adjust for procedure volume. PSI-90 is eleven measures bundled into one.
Healthcare-Associated Infections Standardized Infection Ratios — CLABSI, CAUTI, MRSA, C. diff — 36 measures …to learn what missing data teaches. 43% of NY rows read "Not Available." That's not zero infections.
Maternal Health Cesarean rates, severe maternal morbidity, SM-7: does the hospital screen for maternal depression? …the case where one row is the whole story. SM-7 is yes/no. In NY: 101 yes, 1 no. The model will report "66% yes." The one "no" is the finding.

Round 1 debrief — read the room first

What did the data tell you — and who found it?

  • Which question opened it up? Which one was a dead end?
  • Where did you have to know something the model didn't?
Participants found what they expected — confirmed a hunch, felt smart "Ask without a thesis. See what the data says back."
The model surfaced something they weren't looking for "The model doesn't know what matters. Neither did you yet — and that was the right starting point."
Debate about what a finding means, or who produced it "You brought the question. The model brought a pattern. What you do with that is the work."

Going in without a fixed answer is the method, not a failure of it.

Facilitator only — not on screen

Read this before the workshop — debrief ammunition

Five things the model will get wrong

  • 1ERR volatility on small N (HRRP). Mount Sinai West — ERR 1.26 on 27 cases. Wide confidence interval. Model reports "high-readmission hospital." Won't flag unless pushed.
  • 2Missing data is not random (HAI). 43% of NY rows "Not Available." Smaller, rural, safety-net hospitals disproportionately missing. Model analyzes the 57% that reported.
  • 3PSI-90 is a composite (PSI file). Weighted average of 11 indicators. One PSI can drive the whole score. Model won't decompose unless asked.
  • 4The denominator problem (PSI-06). Pneumothorax doesn't adjust for procedure volume. High rate at a high-volume academic center means something different from a community hospital.
  • 5One answer is the finding (Maternal Health). SM-7: 101 said yes, 1 said no. Model summarizes "66% yes." The 1 is the story. Model won't say that unprompted.

Round 2 · 30 min · self-directed · Gemini free

Interpret — carry your finding into the document

Take what you found in Round 1. One finding — something that surprised you, something you now have a question about. Choose the document at tiny.cc/nyhima → documents that speaks to it. Tell the model your story.

"Here's the data story I found in Round 1. Tell me what this story means in the context of this document."

You're not asking it to summarize the document. You're asking it to apply the document to what you found. Then you judge whether the answer is professionally adequate.

Ahead of the room? Open a second model. Paste the same finding and document. Ask: "A colleague interpreted this for me. What did they miss?"

Round 2 debrief

A model that sounds authoritative about the law is a specific kind of dangerous.

  • If you didn't already know this regulation, how would you know it got it wrong?
  • Would you hand the model's version to a colleague without editing it?

The model applied the framework correctly and reached the wrong conclusion. Why? That's the question. And you're the only one in this room who can answer it.

What did the model explain correctly — and what did it need your expertise to complete?

Round 3 · 25 min · self-directed

Iterate until it's yours

Look at what you have now. Not one thing — a chain. A finding from the data, and an interpretation of what it means against a real regulation. Round 3 is where you make it one piece, and make it yours.

We're not fixing facts. We're fixing voice and shape. Does this read like you made a call, or like you're hedging? Push back. Run it again. Stop when it reads like something you'll put your name on.

Leave on screen · Round 3

Iterate — two paths, pick one

Path A — Iterate the chain you built
BRING Paste both pieces back in — your Round 1 finding AND your Round 2 interpretation. They're one story now.
THINK "Does this read like a decision or a hedge? Does the finding actually connect to what the document says?"
WRITE "Make the connection explicit. Make it sound like I own this." Run it. Push back. Again. One pass is not iteration.
SAVE Run the workbench prompt. Share your Gemini link in your row.
Path B — Re-run the whole loop
Your chain didn't hold? Go back to Round 1. Pick a different finding or a different document. Interrogate → Interpret → Iterate, faster this time. You know the moves now. Land on a story that holds — then run Path A on it.
The back-and-forth is the point. A finding and an interpretation that don't talk to each other aren't a story yet — iteration is what connects them.

Round 3 debrief

Would you put your name on the final version?

  • Where did pushing back actually change the voice — or tighten the connection between the finding and the document?
  • Where did the model just agree with you?
  • Did anyone abandon their first chain and rebuild? What made the second one hold?

That's the move that makes a finding-plus-interpretation into one piece of work that's yours.

For tables that finish early — not required

If you're ahead: two encores

Encore A — Judgment

Pick a scenario at tiny.cc/nyhima → judgment (J1–J4). Write your position first — what should happen and why — before you open the model. Then make the model argue against you. Watch it perform balance when you needed a call.

Encore B — The Documentation Gap

Bring both a data file and a document together from the start. Interrogate (where are we failing?) → Interpret (what does it require?) → Iterate toward a one-page memo to your CEO naming the gap and three actions.

Real work is never just data or just text. It's both — and it takes all three moves to get to something worth acting on.

So — who gets credit for this?

The model interrogated, interpreted, helped you iterate. You decided which questions mattered, what the document actually meant, and when the chain held together. The model did none of that. You did.

It is worth exactly what you put into it.

A vague prompt gets a vague answer. A real question, real expertise, and the patience to iterate — that gets you something you can use. You proved it today. You built one connected thing — yours, from end to end.

You know what to ask. You know "the AI said so" isn't an answer. That's not getting used to it. That's having agency over it.
