Workshop design conversation produced a reformulation of AI literacy centered on effort as the operative variable. The claim: AI output quality scales with the quality of human input — reasoning, iteration, specificity — not with model selection or prompt syntax. Corollary: audiences can detect low-effort AI-assisted work ("slop") without being able to name what's wrong. This reframes AI literacy away from tool competence (knowing how to prompt) toward epistemic investment (having something to say before you open the model). Applied in NYHIMA workshop as three-sprint progression: more models (Sprint 1), more iteration (Sprint 2), your reasoning first (Sprint 3). Closing line encodes the claim directly.
