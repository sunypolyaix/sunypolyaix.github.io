## what you say

Start without slides if you can. Just talk.

"Before we do anything else, I want to ask you a question. Think about the last time AI showed up in your work—not something you went looking for. Something that was already there. A recommendation. A flagged record. A suggested code. A pre-populated field.

Who prompted that?

Most of what gets called AI literacy assumes you're the one asking. You type something, the AI responds, you decide what to do with it. That's one kind of AI. It's not the only kind—and for most of you, it may not even be the main kind.

Today we're going to work with the kind you ask. The compositional kind. Prompts, responses, judgment. Not because it's more important than the AI that's already in your workflows—but because understanding this side makes you better at reading the other side.

By the end of this workshop you'll have worked with at least two different AI models on real HIM scenarios. You'll have made them argue with each other. And you'll have a small workbench—artifacts you generated, saved, and can take back to your organization.

That's the goal. Let's start."

## what you don't say

- Don't explain what an LLM is. They'll see it.
- Don't apologize for the tech. If something breaks, that's data.
- Don't promise the AI will be impressive. It won't always be.

## notes

The phrase "who prompted that?" is the load-bearing move. It plants the directive/compositional distinction without naming it. You'll return to it in the wrap.
