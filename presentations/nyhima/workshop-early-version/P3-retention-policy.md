## situation

Your organization is updating its medical records retention policy for the first time in six years. Legal has asked HIM to lead the review. You need to interpret the relevant federal and state guidance and identify where the policy has gaps.

## source

Federal Baseline (HIPAA):
- HIPAA does not specify how long medical records must be retained.
- HIPAA requires covered entities to retain documentation of policies and procedures for 6 years from creation or last effective date.

CMS Conditions of Participation (42 CFR § 482.24):
- Hospitals must retain medical records for at least 5 years.
- For minors: records must be retained until the patient reaches 21 or for 5 years, whichever is longer.

New York State (example):
- Adult records: 6 years from date of service or 3 years after patient's death
- Minor records: until age 23 or 6 years from last service, whichever is longer
- Diagnostic images: 6 years (adults), until age 23 (minors)

Current organizational policy (excerpt):
"Medical records shall be retained for a minimum of five (5) years from the date of last service. Records for deceased patients may be destroyed after three (3) years."

## writing_prompt

Identify the gaps between our current policy and the applicable requirements. Draft a summary of what needs to change and why, suitable for presenting to our legal and compliance team.
