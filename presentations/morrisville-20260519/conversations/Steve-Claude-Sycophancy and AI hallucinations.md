Both are covered, but not by name — which is worth noting.

**Sycophancy** is the heart of Exercise 3 (Push Back). The explicit language: *"Models are trained to be agreeable. Agreeable is not the same as accurate."* Students practice this by pushing back and watching whether the model defends or folds. The concept is demonstrated through action, not defined as a term.

**Hallucinations** are addressed in Exercise 4 (Trust Audit): students ask the model directly where it shouldn't be trusted, then audit whether its self-assessment matches their skepticism from Exercises 1–3.

**The gap:** neither word appears. A student finishing the permit has *experienced* both phenomena but can't necessarily name them or explain them to someone else.

Whether that's a problem depends on your goal. If the permit is about developing practice, naming isn't required — the behavior is trained in. If students need to discuss AI critically in academic or professional contexts, they need the vocabulary too.

One clean fix: add a brief framing note to Ex3 and Ex4 — a single sentence each that names the thing they're doing. "This is called sycophancy." "Confident false outputs are called hallucinations." Keeps it action-first but gives them the term to carry forward.
