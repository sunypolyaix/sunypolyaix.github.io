Seven failure modes,
one root cause.

Human-factors research on operators working alongside automated systems.

01

Manual skill atrophy

Procedural skills degrade from disuse.

02

Plan continuation bias

Cognitive commitment to the AI-established course despite new cues.

03

Vigilance decrement

Attention drifts from primary indicators during automation.

04

Omission errors

Failing to notice when the automation misses something.

05

Commission errors

Acting on recommendations without independent verification.

06

Confirmation bias

Misinterpreting ambiguous information to confirm the plan.

07

Expectation-driven processing

Seeing what the AI predicted, not what is actually there.

05  ·  What makes this round different vs. earlier automation

Earlier failures
were mechanically legible.
This round is not.

01

The logic is opaque.

The system recommends based on patterns learned from large amounts of data. You cannot always ask why and get a clear answer.

02

It fails by being confidently wrong.

A plausible-looking recommendation that happens to be incorrect for your specific cow, vehicle, or crop. Nothing in the interface signals that.

03

It can increase cognitive demand.

The milking robot reduced physical labor. The AI-assisted system requires constant evaluation of recommendations you cannot fully see into — at scale, every day.

06  ·  The proposal A design principle, not a course
Not: teach students to use the tools. Not: teach students about automation bias.

Design courses where students
practice the moment of judgment
— before compliance.

Dairy
Scenarios where the feeding algorithm has miscalculated — and they catch it by reading the herd.
Auto
Vehicles where the scanner recommendation is wrong — and hands-on inspection is the only thing that catches it.
Horticulture
Disease alerts that are false positives — and silent outbreaks the system missed.
