# AI Literacy in Applied Contexts

**Steven M. Schneider**  
Professor of Information Design & Technology · Co-Director, AIX Center  
SUNY Polytechnic Institute · steve@sunypoly.edu

Talk delivered at SUNY Morrisville Faculty Development · May 19, 2026

---

Most AI literacy pedagogy was built for one scenario: a student prompts a model, receives text, and shapes the output. That is the compositional case. It describes academic writing, legal drafting, business analysis.

It does not describe what nurses, automotive technicians, dairy farmers, or greenhouse managers encounter. For these practitioners, AI is embedded infrastructure that recommends an action before the human has formed an independent assessment. The cognitive demand is not revision — it is judgment at the moment of deployment.

Human-factors researchers have documented seven recurring failure modes when operators work alongside automated systems: manual skill atrophy, plan continuation bias, vigilance decrement, omission errors, commission errors, confirmation bias, and expectation-driven processing. These phenomena are well-studied in aviation, clinical decision support, and process control. The AI literacy literature has not developed pedagogy for them.

The pedagogical response is not instruction about bias. It is repeated, realistic practice in scenarios where the AI recommendation is sometimes wrong by design — where following the system blindly costs students something before it costs them something in the field.

**The question is not whether students can use AI. It is whether they can maintain an independent model of the situation while AI is already organizing what they see.**

## A Note on How This Was Made

One hundred percent of this content is AI-generated — except the prompts.

Prior knowledge of Morrisville's programs, AI literacy pedagogy, and the human-factors literature informed the prompts. The details — the specific failure modes, the greenhouse automation systems, the diagnostic workflows for auto technicians, the clinical decision support literature — were developed through sustained conversation with a language model.

The source conversations are in `conversations/`. The draft history is in `drafts/`. The feedback that changed the argument is in `drafts/feedback/`.

The corpus totals approximately 45,000 words. The finished talk is approximately 900 words.

The ratio is not waste. It is the evidence of composition.

To explore the corpus interactively: open [`morrisville-explore.html`](morrisville-explore.html). To query it manually via a model: see [`query/how-to-query.md`](query/how-to-query.md).
