**Problem 2: Plan continuation bias under automation.** This is the one that directly maps to your framework. Dekker and the aviation safety community have documented something specific: when a pilot has committed to a plan and automation is executing that plan, the pilot becomes **cognitively committed** to the plan. Even when new cues arrive — deteriorating weather, a systems failure, a navigation discrepancy — the pilot continues with the original plan longer than they should.

The mechanism is interesting: the initial plan felt solid. Automation is executing it smoothly. Subsequent cues that suggest "maybe stop now" are filtered through confirmation bias. The pilot interprets them as consistent with the plan rather than contrary to it. And the closer to the destination, the stronger the bias — you've already committed emotionally and operationally.

This is different from simple automation bias (trusting the system blindly). It's about how automation locks in a trajectory cognitively, making the pilot reluctant to abandon the course.
