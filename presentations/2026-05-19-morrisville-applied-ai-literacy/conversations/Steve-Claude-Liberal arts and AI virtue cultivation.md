Three generations, yeah. Humanists feared the printing press would erode memory and judgment. Digital humanists adapted—they said no, computational tools can *augment* close reading, enable new forms of analysis. Now AI humanists face the same skepticism but inverted: not "will we lose memory" but "will we lose agency in judgment itself."

The pattern holds: each wave insists the previous one was right to worry, but wrong about the outcome. The tool doesn't replace judgment—it changes what judgment *looks like* and what it demands. For humanists it was memorization; for digital humanists, it was interpretation; for AI humanists, it's something like... discernment? Knowing when to trust the tool, when to push back, how to read AI output as a text that needs interrogating.

The key difference: AI humanists can't just say "the tool is neutral." You have to teach the *relationship* to the tool as part of virtue itself.
